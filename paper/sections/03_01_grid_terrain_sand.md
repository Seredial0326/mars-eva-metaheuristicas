### 3.1 Base grid, terrain features and boxwork sand

**Grid and features.** The DEM is cropped (never resampled) to the box, plus a 64 m margin so filters have neighbours at the edges. From it we derive the steepest slope (`gdaldem`, Horn), the residual (DEM minus a Gaussian-smoothed DEM, σ = 5 m), roughness (local standard deviation of the residual, 7 × 7 m) and TPI (DEM minus the local mean, 15 × 15 m). Slope has a mean of 16.2°; 12.4 % of the cells are above 30°. The grey and colour orthophotos agree (Pearson 0.953), and the slope matches a NumPy central-difference slope to a median of 0.30°.

**Sand and ridges from the orthophoto (E8).** No pixel-level map of the Gale boxwork exists [@Siebach2014], so a U-Net [@Ronneberger2015] was trained on weak labels generated by rules on the 0.25 m colour orthophoto, with a spatial split fixed before tuning. The labels mark sand by colour (grey-blue and darker than the median) and ridges by watershed divides that agree with a Sato ridge response [@Sato1998]. Two earlier rule sets were dropped (negative results). In a blind point validation (240 points, two passes), the sand map is right 92.5 % of the time when it says sand (95 % CI 84.6–96.5 %), but it finds only about 1 in 9 sand pixels (producer's accuracy 0.110). `sand.tif` is therefore a conservative lower bound. Predicted ridges were not reliable (user's accuracy 0.000–0.150), a negative result. Along Curiosity's route, the driven cells have less mapped sand than the alternatives (0.039 against 0.085 in the boxwork sols), while slope and roughness do not separate them.
