### 3.7 Radiation dose along the EVA

The background rate is the median of the good-quality RAD tissue-equivalent (detector E) observations over the boxwork sols [@RAD-L2]: 156.4 µGy day⁻¹, with a within-day variation of 1.8 %. Detector E is not corrected for the rover's MMRTG, so it is an upper estimate. Dose along a route is the integral of the rate over the E3 clock of the walk, with each station's service time added. Equivalent dose is estimated with the GCR quality factor Q = 3.05 [@Hassler2014]. A solar-particle-event (SEP) alert fires when the rate stays above 1.5 times its trailing 3-day median for 2 consecutive observations. Over 22 months the rule finds exactly one event (2025-11-11, alarm 17 min after onset, peak 1.92 times the baseline, 31.9 µGy excess). The threshold was chosen on these 22 months, with one weak event (1.9×), and is not validated beyond them. During an alert the astronaut takes the fastest way home (R_T), and the dose of that return is reported. The background dose does not change the plan; only a solar storm can, through the return. The EVAs are E5's two tours from the box-centre habitat, timed with the same E3 edges and station service times as the planner, and R_T is E4's return-time raster for the same habitat.
