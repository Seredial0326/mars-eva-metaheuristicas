## Abstract

Astronauts walking on Mars need routes that respect the terrain, the suit's energy and oxygen, the time to get home in an emergency, and the science they came for. We present an EVA planner for a 2 × 2 km box of the boxwork in Gale crater, explored by Curiosity in 2025–2026. On a 1 m grid built from the HiRISE-based DEM [@Parker2016], each move costs metabolic energy and time from Santee (2001) with Mars gravity in the slope term only. An exact planner keeps, at every point of the route, the energy and time needed to walk back (walkback), and an orienteering solver chooses which science stations to visit and in what order. Classic planning rules fail on this terrain: about a third of what a fixed 1.35 km radius calls safe cannot get back in 30 min, and simple baselines call 21–46 % of their goals safe when they are not, against 0 % for the planner. Inside the box, time binds before energy. Curiosity's own route tests the terrain model and also limits it: DEM features do not explain where the rover drove inside the boxwork, and only sand mapped from the 0.25 m orthophoto carries a small learned price. The planner also models radio line of sight to the habitat, which leaves about 41 % of a 120 min tour without direct radio, and an adaptive walkback that learns the astronaut's own consumption during the walk. It runs offline on a local server, with a HUD, an arm-tablet guide and a 3D rehearsal. Everything is tested in simulation and against a rover's traverse, not with astronauts.
