### 3.9 Best time to leave

The Sun's position follows the Mars24 algorithm [@Allison2000], ported from the 3D view. From every vertex of a planned tour, at 1.7 m, the terrain horizon is the highest elevation angle on the DEM up to 400 m in 72 directions. For each departure time (every 15 min of local true solar time), each vertex is reached at its planned time `t_s` and classed as dark (Sun below a smooth horizon), shadowed (Sun below the terrain horizon) or sunlit. Each vertex is weighted by the time until the next one, so a stop counts its service time. The best departure has the largest sunlit share; ties go to the highest lowest Sun. The lit window is the range of departures with the whole walk sunlit. The same light states give E5 its station time windows. For each departure, a station's window is the longest sunlit stretch, sampled every minute until the sol's local midnight, that leaves room for its service, since the work must be done in the light too. A station with no such stretch is dropped for that departure. The tour is then solved with time windows by the same exact solvers for every departure from 05:00 to 19:00 every 15 min. Walking between stations is not limited by the light, and waiting for a window costs 0 W (205 W as an upper bound). Only light is modelled: dust, refraction and shadows from features the DEM misses are not.
