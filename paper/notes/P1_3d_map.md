Owner: Juan (`@JuanCorredor2023`) · Issue: #42; walking freely and the astronaut #109; map layers #110 · PRs: #54 (block 1 of 6), #63 (block 2), #66 (block 3), #67 (block 4), #69 (block 5), #71 (block 6, closing), #117 (#115), #137 (#109), #138 (block 7, map layers) · Status: done (terrain; route, stations and habitat on the terrain; route rehearsal at eye height; Sun of Mars by sol and local time; shadows cast by the relief; a shareable view and picture; regression pass and page README); block 7 adds the 2D planner's map layers draped on the terrain. Rover photos in the 3D view are a separate piece (P3) · Last update: 2026-11-15

## Question
What does the astronaut gain from seeing the planned EVA in 3D before leaving the habitat: the relief of the 2 × 2 km box, the planned route on the ground and a walk-through at eye height? The note follows that order. The first part is how to show a 475 m hillside whose features of interest are 1–2 m high, in a browser, without overselling what the DEM contains.

## Data
- Base grid (JB, `base-grid-v2`): `dem_1m.tif` (2000 × 2000, 1 m) [Parker2016] and `ortho_color_025.tif` (8000 × 8000, 0.25 m) [HiRISE-Gale]. No cell is NaN in the box.
- Nothing is filtered out. The orthophoto is only resized (low preset) or cropped (high preset).

## Method

### Assets for the browser (`src/viz3d/build_terrain_assets.py` → `data/tiles/3d/`)
The output lives inside `data/tiles/` because the API already serves that folder at `/tiles/`: the 3D page needs no change in `api/`. It is **not** part of the `tiles-v2` release (see Reproduce).

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `height_2m.bin` | DEM, mean of each 2 × 2 m block, 1000 × 1000 uint16 (step 7.2 mm) |
| `height_low_2m.bin` | "hillside": DEM smoothed with a Gaussian of σ = 25 m, same grid |
| `detail_2m.bin` | "local relief": Gaussian σ = 3 m of (DEM − hillside), same grid |
| `normal_detail.png` | tangent-space normal map of the local relief, 1000 × 1000 |
| `ortho_4096.jpg` | orthophoto of the whole box at 4096 px (0.49 m/px), low preset |
| `ortho_hi_<r><c>.jpg` | four 4096 px tiles at the native 0.25 m, overlapping 48 m, high preset |
| `terrain.json` | grid, encodings and the numbers quoted below |

- **Heights as numbers, not as an image.** A raw uint16 file is read exactly with one `fetch`; a height PNG would have to hide the value in colour channels, which browsers may alter (colour management, premultiplied alpha).
- **Two presets from the same files.** High: 2 m mesh, 2.0 M triangles, photo at 0.25 m. Low: the 2 m grid averaged to 4 m in the browser, 0.5 M triangles, one 4096 px photo.

### Exaggerating only the local relief
The box is a hillside: elevations span 475 m (−3876 to −3401 m) while knolls and scarps are metres high. A uniform vertical exaggeration would turn the hillside into a wall. The page draws

`shown = height + (k − 1) · detail`

so k = 1 is the DEM itself and k > 1 amplifies only the smoothed local relief. Whenever k ≠ 1 the screen says "Local relief ×k (exaggerated)".

**Why the local relief is smoothed, and why the slider stops at ×5.** At 1 m the DEM minus the hillside is dominated by a pebbly texture and by mosaic seams, not by landforms ([p1_fig1](../figures/p1_fig1_relief_exaggeration.jpg), top row). This agrees with the base grid note: the effective DEM resolution is coarser than 1 m. Exaggerating it exaggerates noise. A 3 m Gaussian keeps knolls, scarps and the lineated outcrop and removes most of the texture (bottom row). Even so, at ×10 half of the box would be shaded as steeper than 39°, so the page offers at most ×5 (`max_exaggeration` in `terrain.json`). Because the exaggeration term uses the smoothed field, the noise itself is never amplified: it stays at ×1 in the geometry.

### Shading: hillside normals plus a normal map
Vertex normals come from the hillside, and the local relief is shaded by a normal map with strength k. Consequences:
- Both presets shade the same, because the normal map does not depend on the mesh spacing.
- Moving the slider only rewrites vertex heights; no normals are recomputed.
- Slopes are stored multiplied by a gain of 4 so that small slopes survive 8 bits (decoding error < 0.02 in slope, test `test_normal_map_decodes_back_to_the_slopes`).

**What the shading shows.** Hillside, knolls and scarps. It does **not** show the boxwork ridges: they are 1–3 m wide and the DEM does not resolve them. In the 3D view the boxwork network is recognised by the HiRISE photo draped on the terrain. The page says so on screen.

### Page (`web/3d/`, three.js r187 [threejs], WebGL)
- A separate page at `/3d/`, served by the existing static mount; three.js is vendored (`web/3d/vendor/`, 8 files, MIT) so the page needs no internet.
- three.js instead of deck.gl's `TerrainLayer` inside the HUD: shadows and terrain draping are experimental in deck.gl 9.1, live exaggeration reloads the mesh there, and a separate page does not collide with the HUD work.
- Scene axes: X east, Y up, Z south, origin at the box centre. CRS coordinates (about 8.1 × 10⁶ m) are shifted in JavaScript (64-bit) before reaching the GPU (32-bit).
- No custom shaders: `MeshStandardMaterial` with the photo and the normal map. In the high preset the four photo tiles share one UV set through per-texture transforms.
- Rendering on demand (only when the camera moves), a compass, and a readout of CRS position and elevation. The compass is a button (face north, also from the keyboard); the round button under it, a frame's four corners, goes back to the opening view (it was a house until #124, which now only means the rehearsal's "Return home").

### Camera: an arcball on the terrain (`web/3d/controls.js`, `web/3d/camera_math.js`)
three.js' stock map controls orbit around a target that drifts away from what the user is looking at, so they were replaced. Every gesture acts on the **terrain point under the cursor**, found by casting a ray against the height grid (step proportional to the height above ground, then bisection):
- **Rotate** (drag, or one finger): the view turns around that point, which stays fixed on screen. The camera has a heading and a tilt but never rolls, so the horizon stays level. A ring marks the point while rotating.
- **Move** (right-drag, Shift + drag, or two fingers): the grabbed point follows the cursor and the camera keeps its altitude.
- **Zoom** (wheel or pinch): towards that point, which stays under the cursor; two-finger twist turns the heading.
- If the cursor is off the terrain, rotation and zoom use the terrain at the centre of the screen (or the box centre), never a point in empty space. The camera stays at least 2 m above the ground. `?drag=pan` swaps the two mouse drags (map style).

### Route, stations and habitat on the terrain (`web/3d/plan_model.js`, `api.js`, `overlays.js`, `markers.js`)
Rule: the page draws what the plan answer contains, as is. It does not plan and does not assume where the route came from; every optional field (profile, turn, goal, totals) may be missing.

- **Where the plan comes from.** The page only reads: `GET /plan/last`, the last plan made in the 2D planner (the server answers `{request, plan}`; the page uses `plan`). **It never plans by itself.** The reason: the server saves every plan it makes as the last plan, and the guide screen on the arm tablet loads that one, so a page that planned on opening would silently replace the astronaut's plan. With no saved plan the page shows the terrain alone and says "No plan yet: plan an EVA in the planner, then press Reload plan". A second button, "Plan with default settings" (or `?plan=default` in the address), sends the 2D planner's own default request when the user asks for it, and says that the result becomes the last plan: the saved stations scored by the rubric, 1000 Wh with a 10 % margin, 120 min, cells over 30° blocked, start at the box centre. If a request fails the terrain still works and the panel gives the server's reason. The panel shows where the plan comes from, its length, time and energy, the answer's messages and a "Reload plan" button. The API writes its messages for the team (`E5 tour: 8 of 13 stations, prize 37 (order R3 > R2 > …)`, `service power assumed 205 W (E4 default)`, source `E5 tour (live, E5 plan_tour (src/orienteering))`); since #124 (block 1) the panel says them in plain words and keeps every number: "8 of 13 stations visited · science value 37 · order R3 → R2 → …", "Left out, they do not fit in the limits: Q1, C1, C2, H1, H3", "Power used at the stations: 205 W (the planner's default)", and "Last plan from the planner" without the engine (`planMessages` in `plan_model.js`). These describe the plan, so they are plain lines; ⚠ in amber is kept for what needs a look: not feasible, no route, a failed request, ignored fields, a point the planner moved, the sample route that is not a plan, and any message the page does not know, shown as the API wrote it. The source stays in `viz3d.plan` for the team.
- **Plan model (no three.js, tested in Node).** The GeoJSON becomes routes, points and an optional per-vertex profile; a profile array is kept only if it has one value per vertex, and a bad field is listed as ignored instead of breaking the page. Each route is split so that no step is longer than 1 m; every new vertex keeps its fractional index into the original vertices and its cumulative 2D length, which is what the rehearsal needs to interpolate the profile and walk the route by time or by distance. The route is cut into outbound and return at `turn_index` when the answer has one.
- **Line draped on the mesh.** The route is drawn with fat lines (`Line2`, 4 px wide at any distance). Besides its own vertices the line gets a vertex wherever it crosses an edge of a terrain triangle (mesh column, row or cell diagonal), and every vertex is placed on the drawn triangles (`Terrain.surfaceAt`) plus 0.3 m. Between two consecutive vertices the ground is a plane, so the whole line, not only its vertices, is 0.3 m above the ground. Heights are recomputed when the relief slider moves.
- **Two legs, two passes.** Outbound in the 2D planner's cyan; the return leg in pink and shifted 0.8 m to the right of the direction of travel (direction taken over ±3 m, shift fading to zero over 4 m at both ends), because an E4 round trip returns along almost the same cells and one leg would hide the other. Each leg is drawn twice: solid with the depth test, and at 30 % opacity without it, so the route can be followed behind a knoll. The 1 m segments overlap on screen, so the faint pass uses the stencil buffer to paint each pixel once; without it the "faint" line adds up to a solid one (numbers in Checks).
- **Markers.** A generic layer takes `{id, x, y, kind, label, color, data}` in CRS metres and draws a pin (label, thin pole, ground dot) as a sprite of constant size on screen, never hidden by the terrain, like a map label. Stations take the 2D planner's colours by type (type from the answer, else from `GET /stations`, else grey); the station the route goes to is highlighted (filled label, taller pole); a station that a tour skipped (`visited: false`) is dimmed. The E5 tour also writes a `stop` point on the route for every station it visits; it gets no pin of its own when it names a station of the plan (block 6: before, each visited station had two pins). The habitat is also a white dome 8 m across standing on the lowest ground under its rim. `pick()` returns the marker under a screen point using the painted pixels of the pin, and a click calls `onSelect` (hook for the station card, and for rover photos in P3).
- **Relief slider** from ×1 to ×5 in steps of 0.5; the page now opens at ×2 (`?k=` changes it). Terrain, route, markers and the camera's floor follow it, at most once per frame.

### Rehearsing the route (`web/3d/rehearse_model.js`, `rehearse.js`, `card.js`)
"Rehearse" walks the planned route in first person before the EVA; "Exit" gives the free-flight view back as it was. The page still plans nothing: it plays the profile of the plan answer.

- **Time base.** The clock is the profile's `t_s` when the answer has one, so the walker moves at the planner's own pace (0.52 to 0.91 m/s on the E4 route). A stop is charged to the step that arrives at it: in the E4 answer `t_s` jumps 601.6 s over the last metre before the goal, which is 1.7 s of walking plus the 10 min of service. The model splits any step that is more than 5 times slower than the median pace, and more than 10 s longer than that, into a walk at the pace of the step before and a wait at its end; during the wait the walker stands on the vertex, energy and O₂ keep rising with the clock and the energy and time to return do not change. With no profile the time is distance at an assumed 1.0 m/s, and the screen says "assumed 1 m/s".
- **Profile values come from the true route**, linearly between its vertices, so at a vertex they are the answer's own numbers. The energy bar (used / needed to return / spare of the usable budget) and its two lines of text repeat the arithmetic of the 2D planner's route profile, in the glossary's words and numbers since #124 block 2 (whole Wh, "spare", "home in … min" in whole minutes).
- **Smoothed camera path, and why.** The planner moves cell to cell in eight directions, so the true path is a staircase that turns 45° at a vertex every metre or two: a camera walked along it shakes. The camera follows a copy of the route averaged along the path with a Gaussian of σ = 2.5 m (sampled every 0.5 m), pinned exactly at the route ends, at the stops and at the turn (the path is continued by its own mirror image through those points, so they do not move), and smoothed less wherever it would be more than 1.2 m from the true route, so it never cuts a corner by more than 1.5 m. Only the camera position and heading use this copy.
- **Heading** is the direction to the point 12 m further along the camera path (the 2D planner's heading arrow looks 12 vertices ahead), never beyond the next stop: looking past the point where the route turns back would spin the view. It is held over the last 3 m before a stop, and while the walker stands there it turns from the arrival to the departure direction. While playing, the view follows a change of heading with a time constant of 1/6 s.
- **Stops.** A stop is a vertex where the walker stands still, a vertex the answer lists (`stop_index` / `visit_order` of a tour), or the vertex nearest to a station that the answer marks as visited or as the goal, when it is within 3 m. Nothing assumes a single goal. Arriving at a stop while playing pauses and shows the station card (id, type label from `GET /stations/rubric`, score, service time, arrival time); play again runs the wait at the playback speed with the walker standing.
- **Camera.** Eye 1.7 m above the shown ground averaged over ±3 m of path (so it does not bob over every 2 m facet), never under 1.6 m above the ground right under it; vertical field of view 60°; near plane 0.3 m instead of the 2 m of free flight. Dragging looks around (offsets from the direction of travel, ±70° of pitch); double-click or "Recentre" looks forward. "Chase view" puts the camera 15 m behind the walker and 8 m above the ground it stands on, at least 2 m above the ground under the camera, with the astronaut of the next section for the walker (until #109, a 1.7 m pink capsule; the astronaut keeps its anchor point, so the chase-view distances of `?check=rehearse` are unchanged). It faces the heading and swings its legs at one stride per second of screen time while playing, whatever the playback speed.
- **True scale is forced, and why.** While rehearsing, the relief slider is locked at ×1 and says so; the previous value comes back on exit. The exaggeration is a reading aid from above. At eye height it would put a wall where there is a knoll, and the slopes the astronaut will walk are what the rehearsal is for.
- **Player.** Since #124 (block 2) the side panel is hidden while rehearsing, so the scene shows, and "Panel" in the player opens and closes it without leaving (a class of its own on the page body: the photo view's would also hide the map layer). On top, the next maneuver as the arm tablet shows it: the arrow, the distance big ("350 m", "Now") and the instruction under it. Then one row of main buttons: play / pause, "Return home", "Off route", "Solar storm"; then "More", "Panel" and "Exit", in the same reading and Tab order. Under "More": "Chase view", "Recentre", ×1, ×30, ×60 (default) and ×180, voice, the "Use ×k" demo and the two lines of energy numbers. Then a scrubber over the whole time of the route walked, the clock line, the panel's Sun line (the local solar time that follows the EVA clock, how high the Sun is and where the light comes from, and "Following the EVA clock: …", copied from the Sun section) and the energy bar. The clock line is the EVA clock, "EVA time of total · outbound or returning · metres"; a way home or a rejoin is a new route that starts where the walker was, but the EVA clock goes on and the way home is a stretch of it ("EVA 102:04 · return home 2:02 of 2:51 · 120 of 160 m"; after a rejoin, "of" the EVA's new end). The solar storm alert also shows beyond the player, as page elements: a red band across the top with the tablet's banner ("SOLAR STORM · return home now") and a red vignette over the view; nothing is added to the 3D scene (the sky is repainted every frame, and `?check=rehearse` compares the scene). Frames are drawn only while playing, dragging or easing. Esc closes what is on top, the card or the message box, one per press, and pauses; it never leaves the rehearsal, because leaving drops the end summary and the place reached: only "Exit" leaves (#124). The page's other Esc keys are unchanged: the message box closes on Esc in any mode, and Esc leaves the photo view and the free walk.
- **Walkback lines on pause.** As in the 2D planner, pausing asks `POST /walkback` for the 30 min return line (red) and the energy-safe boundary (cyan) from the walker's position with the energy left (usable − used, rounded as the 2D planner does), with the plan's own slope limit and habitat; the lines are draped on the mesh like the route and removed on play or exit. This request computes and answers only: `api/main.py` `walkback()` writes no file. The line under the player gives the answer in the tablet's words since #124, in whole minutes and Wh: "Return home from here: ✔ 3 min (limit 30) · ✔ 14 Wh, 869 Wh spare after it (red line: 30 min from home · cyan line: energy-safe area)".
- **Words and numbers** (#124 block 2, #166). The 3D takes its names, state words and number formats from the team's glossary, `web/terms.js`, as the HUD, the arm tablet and the wrist card do: "Home in · limit 30", "Off route", "Rehearse", "Planner", OK / LOW / TURN BACK on every meter, whole minutes and Wh everywhere, and m:ss only on the player's clock line.
- **At the habitat** the rehearsal stays on (#124): leaving would drop the end summary, the replay and the Sun of the end. The storm alert and RETURN NOW end there; the player shrinks to the maneuver ("Home"), "↺ Replay" (the Play button, also Space outside controls), "Summary", the scrubber and the clock; the card "Plan against the walk" (also sent to `POST /walks` for the debrief) has "Replay", which starts again from the start of the plan as a new walk with its own summary, and "Exit". Going back with the scrubber gives the full player; arriving again on the same walk shows the same card, without posting again.
- **`flyTo`** (`controls.js`): an eased flight of about 1 s to a pose given as CRS position, eye height above the ground, heading, pitch and field of view. Clicking a pin in free flight shows the same card with "Fly here", which views the station from 60 m. A pose may be lower than 2 m and may look above the horizon (rover photo poses, P3); the next gesture brings the free-flight limits back.

### Walking freely and the astronaut (`web/3d/walk_model.js`, `walk.js`, `astronaut.js`; issue #109)
The rehearsal plays the plan on its own clock; "Walk freely" lets the astronaut walk anywhere in the box themself, like a game, to look at a slope, a station or the way back from any side. It plans nothing and records nothing.
- **Controls.** W/S or Up/Down walk forward/back, A/D step sideways, and Left/Right turn (by `KeyboardEvent.code`, so the same keys on any layout); a click captures the mouse (Pointer Lock API) and moving it turns the view, 0.0025 rad per pixel, pitch within ±85°; the first Escape frees the mouse, a second one leaves. Where the browser refuses to capture it, dragging looks around (the hint says so). Shift (or "Run") runs, F (or "Fast") toggles fast travel, C (or "Chase view") shows the astronaut from 6 m behind and 2.5 m above its ground, at least 2 m above the ground under the camera. On a screen whose main pointer is a finger (`hover: none` and `pointer: coarse`) an arrow pad is shown, held to walk or step sideways, and a drag looks around as in the rehearsal. Space (rehearsal play) and the photo view's Escape are left alone.
- **Keyboard turning (#124).** `walk_model.js` applies `TURN_SPEED = Math.PI / 2` rad/s (90°/s, a quarter turn in one second) to the current yaw using each frame's elapsed time, capped as for walking. This gives time to aim without a mouse; it is a control choice, not a measured usability result. Turning precedes movement, so Up + Left follows a curve. It adds to mouse look without resetting yaw or changing pitch, opposite arrows cancel, and releasing the arrow stops the turn.
- **Keyboard turning checks.** `tests/viz3d/walk_model.test.mjs` checks the mapping, elapsed-time scaling, curves at each walking speed, release and composition with mouse look; test 30 of `tests/viz3d/ui_controls.test.mjs` requires A/D to strafe and Left/Right to be bound to turning. The local `node --test tests/viz3d/ tests/web/` run reported 263 passed and one failed on stdout: the existing rehearsal test at `tests/viz3d/rehearse_model.test.mjs:342` compares `home in 0.0 min` with the guide's `home in 0 min`. An isolated run of that test also fails; its files were not changed. Running just the walk model and UI controls tests reported 66 passed. `?check=walk` is unchanged: its synthetic keys, touch pad and drag retain their meanings; it does not exercise the new turn keys. Browser and real Pointer Lock testing were not run for this keyboard change.
- **Speeds and the eye.** 1 m/s walking, the pace the rehearsal assumes when a plan has no profile; 3 m/s running; 20 m/s fast travel, to cross the box in under two minutes. A diagonal is not faster. Speeds are horizontal (on the map, not along the slope). A frame counts for at most 0.1 s. The eye is 1.7 m above the drawn ground under it (`Terrain.surfaceAt`), the rehearsal's `EYE_HEIGHT`; the relief is locked at ×1, as in the rehearsal and for the same reason.
- **Where one may walk.** 1 m inside the box edge (there is no ground past it) and outside the habitat dome: the walker is a disc of 0.3 m radius pushed straight out of the 4 m dome. A move is cut into steps of at most 0.5 m, each one constrained, so fast travel cannot jump through the dome. Rocks, slopes and the planner's blocked cells do not stop the walker.
- **Start.** Next to the habitat, 1.5 m from the dome wall, facing the first route vertex more than 2 m past that ring (the way the route leaves); with no plan, at the ground point in the middle of the screen, with the camera's heading.
- **The route as a walker sees it.** The route lines of free flight (4 px, seen through knolls) are hidden while walking. Each leg is laid on the ground instead as a strip 0.8 m wide in its colour, 0.08 m above the drawn surface plus a depth offset, built on the draped vertices of the line (`overlays.js`, one per mesh-edge crossing) with mitred corners capped at twice the half width. Each station gets a ring (2.2 to 2.8 m radius, draped the same way) and a 2.4 m pole with a flag in its colour; the pins stay. The readout says the speed and the distance to the nearest leg ("Walking 1.0 m/s · 12 m from the route").
- **The astronaut** (`astronaut.js`) is 19 three.js primitives, 2264 triangles, no model or texture: white suit, grey boots and gloves, a backpack, a helmet with a gold visor at 1.70 m and bands of the 2D planner's astronaut pink. It is 1.85 m tall, 0.73 m wide, lit by the Sun with a little emissive light so its shaded side is never black, and casts no shadow. Legs and arms swing from hip and shoulder pivots; in this mode the swing follows the distance walked (one stride per 1.4 m, ±0.3 rad, so the stiff legs lift the feet by under 5 cm).
- **Leaving** puts back the camera pose, field of view, near plane, relief, the route lines and the photo pins, and removes every listener and object the mode added. Reloading the plan leaves the mode (its path would be of the old route).

### The Sun of Mars (`web/3d/mars_sun.js`, `sun_model.js`, `sun.js`)
The scene is lit from where the Sun is at the box centre at a Curiosity sol and a local time, so the astronaut sees which slopes will be lit or dark during the EVA. Until block 4 the light was fixed (from the west, 35° high).

- **Algorithm.** Mars24 [Allison2000], written from the equation list of the GISS page (steps A-1 to D-6): days since J2000 in Terrestrial Time, mean anomaly, fictitious mean Sun, seven planetary perturbation terms, equation of centre, areocentric solar longitude Ls, equation of time, Coordinated Mars Time, local mean and local true solar time, subsolar point, solar declination, and from them the zenith angle and azimuth at a site. The module has no dependencies and is tested in Node. It exists only in JavaScript: the figure and the numbers of this note are made by running the same file with Node (`src/viz3d/sun_table.mjs`), not by a second implementation.
- **Conventions, and the traps.** (1) The page is west-positive; the module takes longitudes **east**, as the CRS gives them (longitude = x / R, latitude = y / R on the sphere of R = 3,396,190 m), and converts inside. (2) TT − UTC is a parameter: by default 32.184 s plus the leap seconds in force at the date (table from 1999; 64.184 s for the worked examples of 2000 and 2004, 69.184 s since 2017). (3) Mars24 latitudes are planetographic and the CRS ones planetocentric: the site latitude is converted (−4.799° becomes −4.856°, flattening from radii 3396.19 and 3376.20 km). (4) Azimuth is the compass bearing of the Sun, from north through east; the unit vector towards the Sun in the scene (X east, Y up, Z south) is (sin A cos e, sin e, −cos A cos e). (5) Curiosity's mission clock is the local mean solar time at 137.42° E, the planned landing site, with sol 0 the sol of landing (Mars Sol Date 49269); the box centre is at 137.386° E.
- **The clock of the page is local true solar time at the box centre** (12:00 is the Sun on the meridian), because that is what tells where the light comes from. The sol is counted on the same clock (`trueSolarDate`: Mars Sol Date + longitude / 360 + equation of time / 360 − 49269), so the time slider has no jump at any hour. This sol number is the mission's except within the equation of time of midnight: on sol 4711 the equation of time is +37.5 min, so 15:00 here is 14:23 on the mission clock, and the first 37 min after true midnight still belong to mission sol 4710. The inverse (sol and local true solar time to UTC) is solved by Newton steps on that count.
- **In the page.** A "Sun" section in the panel: sol (number), local solar time (slider over the 24 h in 5 min steps) and a readout in plain words, e.g. "Sun 44° high, light from the west", plus the Earth date. The exact height, the compass bearing (azimuth) and the season (Ls) are in the readout's tooltip; until #124 (block 1) the line itself said "Sun 44° above the horizon · light from the west (azimuth 282°) · Ls 167°". Default: **sol 4711 at 15:00**; `?sol=4711&lt=15:00` in the address changes it. `?light=az,el` remains as a debug override that fixes the light as before and switches the Sun off.
- **Start at the plan's best time to leave** (#124, block 1). Once a plan is drawn, the page asks `GET /plan/departure?sol=<sol shown>` (P6, `api/departure.py`: the *Best time to leave* line of the 2D planner; about 1 s by the timing of the P6 note, and it saves nothing) and moves the start to the answer's best departure (`best.start_ltst_h`, to the minute): 11:00 for the planner's 120 min tour on sol 4711, where the HUD says "best 11:00". The page does not wait for it, and the note says "Starts at the best time to leave, 11:00, as the planner suggests". The start stays as it was with `?lt=` or `?light=`, with `?check=` (a check must not depend on the planner), without a plan, when the answer gives no usable time (404 without a plan, 422 for a route with no clock, no server, another sol, or a best departure with no sunlight at all), and when the Sun was moved before the answer came (the sol or time in the panel, a rehearsal, a photo view); in the last case the note only says "The planner suggests leaving at 11:00". The planner's clock is local true solar time at the habitat and the page's at the box centre: inside the box they differ by 4 s at most. A view link carries the start as `lt=`, so it opens at the same time without asking again.
- **Light levels are a look, not a radiometric model.** The directional light keeps its previous strength (2.6) with the Sun above 12° and fades smoothly to zero at the horizon (the planet itself casts no shadow in the scene, so a Sun under the horizon must light nothing). The sky (hemisphere) light goes from 1.1 by day to 0.3 at night between +6° and −6°, never black, and the sky colour darkens with it; below the horizon the panel says "Night: the Sun is 45° below the horizon" (with the depth of that moment). No atmosphere, dust opacity, twilight or sky brightness is computed.
- **While rehearsing the route** the Sun advances with the EVA clock from the chosen start (UTC of the start + elapsed seconds of plan time); the panel shows the current local time and its controls are locked. Leaving the rehearsal goes back to the start time.
- **"Relief only"** hides the photo and shows plain grey ground, to judge the light without the shadows that the photo already carries; unticking it puts back the same textures and colours (one in the low preset, four in the high one).
- **Not modelled:** the horizon beyond the box (a hill outside it between the site and the Sun), refraction, the size of the solar disc. Shadows cast inside the box are the next subsection. One Sun direction is used for the whole 2 km box (its edges are 0.017° of longitude from the centre, 4 s of time).

### Cast shadows (`web/3d/shadow_fit.js`, `shadows.js`)
The sun light casts shadows with three.js' own shadow maps (r187, no custom shader): the scene's depth seen from the Sun is drawn into a texture and every lit pixel of the terrain is compared with it. The terrain casts and receives; the habitat dome casts; pins and route lines do neither. The agreed reference for the direction is this and no more: HiRISE takes its images at about 15:00 local time, so at 15:00 the shadows of the 3D map must fall towards the east, as in the photo.

- **The camera of the shadow map covers the whole block.** It is orthographic and looks along the sunlight. Its box is the bounding box of the block seen from the Sun (2 × 2 km, from the lowest to the highest shown height plus 5 m for the dome) with 4 m of margin across and 10 m in depth, computed by a function with no three.js (`fitShadowBox`, tested in Node). At 15:00 the box is 2387 × 2004 m; at 07:00 and 17:00 it is 2234 × 1026 m.
- **Map size and what one texel covers.** 4096 × 4096 texels in the high preset, 2048 × 2048 in the low one. Across the Sun's azimuth a texel covers 0.55 to 0.58 m of ground (high) or 1.09 to 1.17 m (low). Along the azimuth it is stretched by 1 / sin(elevation): 0.71 m at 15:00 and 1.01 m at 07:00 and 17:00 (high), 1.41 m and 2.01 m (low). The filter is three.js' PCF (five taps within one texel), so a shadow edge is about two texels soft.
- **Drawn on demand, not every frame** (`renderer.shadowMap.autoUpdate = false`). The map is drawn again when the Sun has moved more than 0.2° since it was drawn (a 100 m shadow moves 0.35 m, under one texel), when the relief slider changes the heights, when the plan changes (the dome), and when the switch is turned on. Between redraws the old map is used with the camera it was drawn with. Below the horizon nothing is drawn: the sun light is zero there.
- **Why sunlit ground shows no acne.** The depth map holds the faces turned away from the Sun (three.js' default for a one-sided material). A point in shadow on a height field always has such a face between it and the Sun, and sunlit ground is never compared with itself, which is the usual cause of acne. The remaining guard is a depth bias of 1.5 texels (0.82 to 0.87 m along the light in the high preset, 1.63 to 1.75 m in the low one): ground closer than that to a point does not shade it. `normalBias` stays 0. The dome casts with its sunlit side instead, so the bias leaves no gap behind it.
- **Switch.** A "Shadows" checkbox in the Sun section, on by default in both presets; `?shadows=0` starts without them. Off is the page of block 4: a light that casts no shadow compiles the same programs as before.
- **The shadows are those of the relief as shown.** They are true only at relief ×1; at ×2 to ×5 they are the shadows of the exaggerated surface. They show knolls and scarps, not the 1–2 m boxwork ridges, which the DEM does not resolve. The page says so in "About this view".

### A view to share: address and picture (`web/3d/view_link.js`, `main.js`)

The position is absolute, not a height above the ground, because the shown ground moves with the relief slider and the camera does not. ### Map layers of the 2D planner on the terrain (`web/3d/map_layers_model.js`, `map_layers.js`)
Block 7 (issue #110). The layers of the 2D planner draped on the 3D relief, so that where the radio reaches, the cells too steep to cross, sand, walking cost and boulders are seen against the shape of the ground. The 2D HUD is the reference: the same images and URLs, the same legend colours and words, the same opacity slider. No endpoint and no contract changed.

- **In the page.** A "Map layer" section in the panel: a select, Off by default, with Radio coverage (E11), Slope, Too steep to cross, Sand detected (E8), Walking cost (E3) and Boulders (E9). The codes are for the team: since #124 (block 1) the interface shows the HUD's plain names only, and hover texts that are the HUD's words without its codes or the boulder network's name; an opacity slider mapped as in the 2D (opacity = value^2.2) that starts where the 2D starts (1 for radio, steep, sand and boulders, whose alpha is in the PNG; 0.75 for cost; 0.5 for slope); the 2D's legend and note under it. One layer at a time.
- **Images.** `/layers/comms.png?x=&y=` with the plan's habitat (else its start point, else the box centre); `/layers/blocked.png?max_cell_slope_deg=` with the plan's own limit (30 by default; a plan that blocks no cell gives "The plan does not block steep cells."); `/layers/sand.png`, `/layers/cost.png` and `/layers/rocks.png`. Each is one 2000 × 2000 image of the box, row 0 north, one pixel a 1 m cell. The slope is not a `/layers` PNG, in the 2D either: its XYZ tiles listed by `GET /layers` (64 tiles at zoom 3, 1 m) are put together on one 2000 × 2000 canvas, as the 2D shows them. The note under the legend comes from each image's info JSON (`/layers/comms`, `/layers/blocked`, …) in the 2D's words; an error answer (404 without the sand or boulder data, 503 for the radio without GDAL, 422) reads "Unavailable: …". Nothing is requested until a layer is picked, so the page and its other checks are as before; a reloaded plan with another habitat or limit loads the radio or steep layer again.
- **Drawn as an unlit overlay.** A group of its own, `map-layer`, in the scene, not inside the terrain's group (the relief, the shadows and the checks read that group as terrain only). One mesh per terrain mesh, sharing its geometry, so the layer follows the relief slider with nothing to refresh, and its UVs (u east, v north; with row 0 north in the PNGs the texture is flipped, `flipY`). `MeshBasicMaterial`: the colours are the 2D's, and the shaded relief and the cast shadows of the terrain show through the opacity; the layer neither casts nor receives shadows.
- **The route stays as it was.** The layer is drawn in the opaque pass after the terrain and the lines (`renderOrder` 1, `CustomBlending` with `transparent: false`, no depth write) and at the terrain's own depth (the terrain's polygon offset): it tints only the terrain pixels left visible, so the route, its faint pass behind hills, the station pins, the dome and the photo pins look as without it (numbers in Checks).
- **Memory.** Picking another layer frees the previous texture first (2000 × 2000 RGBA, 16 MB before mipmaps, by arithmetic); a load that ends after another pick is thrown away.
- **With the other modes.** Hidden in the photo view (`body.photo-view`), which compares the rover photo with the bare terrain, and shown again on exit; on during the rehearsal; "Relief only" hides the photo and keeps the layer.
- **Not draped:** THEMIS night temperature (81 m pixels), climate, minerals and dose (panels in the 2D, not maps of the box). The photo from orbit and the shaded relief are the 3D's own.

reuse the pure list helpers and the documented keyboard path in the product and demo.

## Limitations
- The DEM does not resolve the boxwork ridges; neither the geometry nor the shading shows them. Only the photo does.
- The local relief is a smoothed field: at k > 1 small real features below about 6 m across are not exaggerated, and what is exaggerated is no longer to scale. Shading is exaggerated with it.
- The photo carries its own illumination (HiRISE images are taken in the mid-afternoon); the synthetic light is added on top.
- **The light levels are a look.** There is no atmosphere or dust in the model: no opacity, no sky brightness, no twilight colours, no reddening at a low Sun. The fade of the sun light under 12° and the dimming of the sky around the horizon are chosen by eye; brightness on screen is not irradiance.
- **Only the box casts shadows** and the horizon is that of a smooth planet. Ground that terrain outside the 2 × 2 km box would shade is drawn lit, which matters most near its edges at 07:00 and 17:00, and sunrise and sunset do not account for the terrain around the box (the hillside keeps rising beyond its south-east corner).
- **A shadow texel is as large as the ridges.** One texel of the shadow map covers 0.55 to 1.0 m of ground in the high preset and 1.1 to 2.0 m in the low one, and the mesh under it has 2 m or 4 m cells from a DEM that does not resolve the 1–2 m boxwork ridges. The shadows are those of knolls and scarps. A shadow thinner than about two texels fades out, so the render shades slightly less ground than the computation (21.2 % against 22.9 % at 07:00), and ground closer than the bias (0.8 m high preset, 1.7 m low) to what would shade it stays lit.
- **Shadows follow the relief as shown.** At ×2 to ×5 they are the shadows of the exaggerated surface, far larger than the real ones (at 15:00, 6.2 % of the block at ×5 against 0.08 % at ×1). Only ×1 is true, and the page opens at ×2.
- **Shadows appear twice with the photo on.** The photo already carries the shadows of its own mid-afternoon Sun, including those of ridges and boulders that the DEM does not have; the computed ones are drawn over it. "Relief only" shows the computed ones alone.
- **The 15:00 direction rests on little shaded ground at true scale.** At ×1 the DEM shades 0.08 % of the block at 15:00: 17 pixels of the check (one in the low preset), plus the dome's shadow. The east direction has more ground to stand on at ×5 (3156 pixels, 98.9 % of them also shaded by the computation with the true Sun) and at 17:00. The rendered shadows were not compared with the shadows seen in the HiRISE photo or with any catalogue.
- **Shadows are hard and binary in the model.** No penumbra from the solar disc, no light scattered into shadows beyond the constant sky light, and the dome receives no shadow. The independent calculation of the check shares the mesh and the bias rule with the render (it is another algorithm, not other data) and samples its ray every half mesh cell.
- **Cost was measured on one laptop.** By arithmetic, not measured, the 4096 map takes about 130 MB of GPU memory (depth plus the colour target three.js attaches) and the 2048 one about 32 MB; whether a tablet holds 30 fps with shadows was not measured, so "on by default in the low preset" rests on this laptop.
- **Two lights at once.** The photo keeps the shadows of its own mid-afternoon Sun under the computed one, so with the photo on a morning view shows both. "Relief only" is the honest view of the computed light and of the computed shadows.
- **Shading is exaggerated with the relief.** The normal map is scaled by the relief factor, so at the default ×2 slopes look lit and dark as if they were twice as steep; the east-west contrast measured above is for ×2. At true scale (and always while rehearsing) it is the DEM's own.
- The Sun's position was checked against the two worked examples of the Mars24 page and by its own geometry (meridian at noon, east and west), not against the Mars24 program or SPICE for sol 4711. The mission's clock is 2.9 s behind the one computed here, cause not established. Leap seconds are a table up to 2017: one added later would shift everything by 1 s until the table is updated.
- Planetocentric and planetographic latitude are not mixed: the CRS latitude is converted before use. The difference is 0.057° at the box; "up" in the scene is the DEM's vertical, and the two verticals differ by that same angle, which is ignored.
- Close to midnight the Sun passes less than 1° from straight down at this site and season, so its azimuth there swings by 180° within minutes; it lights nothing then.
- DEM mosaic seams are visible as straight lines in the raw local relief (fig. 1, top row); the smoothing attenuates them but does not remove them.
- At eye height the ground looks blurred: the photo has 0.25 m pixels and the eye is 1.7 m from it, and the 2 m facets of the mesh show. The rehearsal gives the lie of the land (slopes, horizon, where the route goes), not the surface underfoot; real Curiosity images (P3) address that.
- The walker's position is simulated: it is the plan played back on its own clock, not a position of the astronaut. With no profile even the pace is an assumption (1 m/s).
- The camera does not walk the planner's cells: its path is up to 0.8 m (E4 route) or 1.1 m (fixed route) from the true route, and nothing checks that the smoothed path avoids a blocked cell the route went around. Times, distances and energy are those of the true route.
- A stop's waiting time is inferred from the profile (a step far slower than its neighbours); a real step that slow would be shown as a short walk and a wait. At a stop with no waiting time the heading changes at once and only the on-screen easing softens it.
- The fixed route was tested only with its first 400 vertices: getting the whole route needs a `POST /plan`, which would replace the saved plan.
- The walkback lines come from `POST /walkback`, whose return energy differed by 2 % from the plan's own `rv_wh` at the point tested; the first request for a habitat takes several seconds. The radiation dose that the 2D profile text adds is not shown here.
- With the near plane at 0.3 m the depth buffer is coarser far away; whether the route line flickers against the ground at long range from eye height was not measured.
- The look of the first-person and chase views was judged from numbers (sky fraction, marker pixel) and one small capture, not from a careful visual review. Frames per second are from one laptop in an emulated viewport.
- After a `flyTo` pose that looks above the horizon, the first gesture tilts the view down to the free-flight limit (2°) and lifts the camera to 2 m.
- Performance is measured on one laptop; no tablet was available.
- The route shown is the planner's path on the 1 m grid, drawn as is: its steps in eight directions look like a zigzag from close up. The 0.8 m shift of the return leg is a drawing aid, not where the astronaut would walk, and beyond roughly 700 m (in a view 800 px high) it is under one pixel, so the two legs merge.
- Pins have the same size at any distance and are never hidden by the terrain: they give no cue of distance, and labels of stations a few metres apart (R0 and H0 are 10 m apart) overlap until the camera is close.
- "Plan with default settings" makes the server save its answer as the last plan (`data/plans/last_plan.json`): the guide screen will load it, and the next visit shows it as "last plan from the planner". The button says so, and the page never does it unasked.
- The multi-station tour was tested with one real answer, made in-process from the E5 PR branch at commit 474b4fd, not through a running server and not with other limits or station sets. E5 is not merged: if its answer changes shape before it is (the `stop` points, `stop_index`, `visit_order`), the fixture has to be made again. A stop's arrival time is inferred from the profile and differs by up to 0.5 s from the one E5 writes, which the page does not read. The default request sends all 13 saved stations, which this version of E5 accepts; a solver with a lower station limit would refuse it, and the page then shows the reason and the terrain only.
- The legs of a tour are drawn as "outbound" up to the last station and "return" from there, the turn the answer gives; the order of the visits is in the cards, not on the line.
- The plain words of the plan's messages come from matching the API's exact sentences (`PLAN_MESSAGES` in `plan_model.js`). If the API rewords one, the panel shows it as the API wrote it, with ⚠, until the list is updated: nothing is lost, but jargon can come back. The panel follows the HUD for the four messages the HUD also keeps out of its warnings; it also shows the tighter-limit and re-plan messages as plain lines, which the HUD shows as warnings.
- A view link carries the camera, relief, sol, time and the shadows switch, not "Relief only" nor the plan: the same address shows another route after the saved plan changes. The saved image is the canvas alone, at screen resolution.
- The regression pass used synthetic events in one browser (Chromium) on one laptop; no real touch screen was used, and `?plan=default` was not opened.
- **Walking freely is a game, not a model of the EVA.** It walks through rocks and up any slope at the same speed (neither the plan's slope limit nor the E3 cost applies), its speed is on the map, not along the ground, and its distance to the route is to the drawn legs (the return leg is the one shifted 0.8 m). `?check=walk` drives the mode with synthetic key, pointer and drag events. A captured mouse cannot be faked, and the app's embedded browser refused to capture it, so mouse capture (Pointer Lock) is untested; looking by dragging was tried with real input. The arrow pad was tried in a phone emulation only, not on a real touch screen. The ground strip lies on the draped centre line: across a ridge narrower than the strip it may dip a few centimetres under the surface.
- **Map layers: the slope legend shows the tile colours.** Until #149 the 2D legend (#2e9b3e, #f2d43a, #f28c28, #d7301f in `web/index.html`) was not the ramp the tiles are coloured with (`SLOPE_RAMP` in `src/gis/tiles.py`: #1a9850, #fee08b, #f46d43, #a50026 at 0, 15, 30 and 45°, and (80, 0, 20) at 90°). #149 moved the 2D legend to that ramp and the 3D copy followed: `SLOPE_LEGEND_RAMP` is now the tile ramp from 0 to 45°, and the Node test asks for it. The legend still ends at "45°+", so it does not show the darker red the tiles reach at 90°.
- The hatch swatch of the steep cells is dark on the dark panel (the same in the 2D sidebar).
- Cells of 30.00 to 30.07° (0.08 % of the box) are blocked by the steep layer but coloured as 30° by the slope tiles: the two layers disagree on them by 8-bit colour, not by geometry.
- One layer at a time, while the 2D stacks several. The steep limit is the plan's, not the 2D's slider. The radio antenna stands at the plan's habitat, else its start point, else the box centre: the 2D's fallback, a base the user sets, does not exist in 3D. Night temperature, climate, minerals and dose are not draped. A view link does not carry the layer.
- The layer is unlit: where an image is opaque (slope, cost) and the slider is at 1, its colour hides the shading of the relief under it.
- A few route pixels at depth ties with the ground are still tinted (5 of 2826 in the low preset, 3 in the high one, one view each).
- The check compares the layer from straight above at one pixel per cell. How it looks at a slant or from far away (mipmaps, anisotropic filtering) was not measured, nor the frames per second with a layer on.
- **Panel of #124 block 2, not seen in a browser.** How much of a 375 × 812 screen the panel covers with *Plan* open was not measured (by a rough CSS sum, about two thirds on a touch phone, against about a quarter with every section closed before); the 44 px sizes and the touch sliders' thumb are by CSS arithmetic; no real touch screen was used. With the photo stops off at the start, `?check=stations` reports `photo_pins_hidden` true whether or not the rehearsal hides them (the check would have to tick the box first), and `?check=photos` leaves the stops on, as opening any photo does. By reading the code no other field of their JSON changes; neither was run for this change.

## What other pieces can use
- `data/tiles/3d/terrain.json` and the three height grids: a ready 2 m DEM, its smooth hillside and its local relief (any browser view; P3, Juan).
- The hillside / local relief split (σ = 25 m and 3 m) and fig. 1 as evidence of the DEM's pixel-scale noise (E8, JB; E3 slope-scale discussion, Sebas).
- `web/3d/terrain.js`: `toLocal`/`toMars` and `heightAt` for anything drawn on the terrain (P3).
- `web/3d/plan_model.js` (no dependencies): reads any `/plan` answer, densifies the route to 1 m keeping the index into the profile. `web/3d/markers.js`: any list of points as pins, with `pick()` and `onSelect` (P3 rover photos).
- `web/3d/rehearse_model.js` (no dependencies but the glossary `web/terms.js`): the state of a walker at any time of a plan (position, heading, stops, interpolated profile) and the split of a stop into walk and wait (guide screen, Sebas). `web/3d/card.js`: `showCard({title, subtitle, rows, note, media, actions})` and `controls.flyTo({x, y, eyeHeight, heading, pitch, fov})` for rover photo poses (P3).
- `web/3d/shadow_fit.js` (no dependencies): the orthographic box that holds a terrain block seen from a given Sun direction, and the ground size of a shadow texel. The ray march of `?check=shadows` (`web/3d/checks.js`, over `Terrain.surfaceAt`) answers "is this ground point in the Sun at this sol and time?" for the drawn mesh: a start for sunlit or shaded stations (E5) or solar exposure (E12), still inside the check.
- `web/3d/mars_sun.js` (no dependencies): Ls, equation of time, local mean and true solar time, Sun elevation and azimuth anywhere on Mars for a UTC instant, Curiosity sol and LMST of an instant and back. Usable from Node (`src/viz3d/sun_table.mjs` prints a sol as JSON): time of day of a rover photo (P3), daylight left for an EVA (E4, E5), solar exposure (E12).

- `web/3d/view_link.js` (no dependencies) and `?view=`: an address that opens the 3D map on an exact view, to link a place from the 2D planner, the guide screen or a figure caption; `viz3d.capture()` gives the picture as a PNG blob.
- `tests/viz3d/fixtures/plan_tour_e5.json` and `src/viz3d/make_tour_fixture.py`: a real `/plan` answer of the E5 tour and the way to make another without a server and without replacing the saved plan (HUD and guide tests, Sebas; E5, Diana).
- `web/3d/walk_model.js` (no dependencies): nearest point of a polyline with the distance along it, a flat strip along a polyline with capped mitres, and box and obstacle limits for a walker. `web/3d/astronaut.js`: an astronaut figure with a heading and a walk cycle for any three.js view (guide screen, the video #123).
- `web/3d/README.md`: how to run and use the page.
- `web/3d/map_layers_model.js` (imports only `api.js`, tested in Node): the 2D planner's layers as data (URLs, legends asserted against `web/index.html`), the slope tiles of the box placed on one canvas, and image comparisons (`compareRGBA`, `bestShift`, `premultiply`, `slopeFromColor`, masks) for any `/layers` image on the 1 m cells with row 0 north (HUD and API, Sebas; tiles, JB).
- `?check=layers`: a regression check that the `/layers` images of E11 radio (JB), E8 sand (JB), E3 cost (Sebas), E9 boulders (Aaron) and the steep cells (API, Sebas) still cover the box on the right cells. A new 2000 × 2000 box image of the same kind appears in the 3D map with its entry in `LAYERS` and its URL in `layerRequest`, and is then checked with no change to the check.
- For the HUD (Sebas): the slope legend shows the tile colours since #149; the 3D copy follows it, and `tests/viz3d/map_layers_model.test.mjs` fails if the two drift apart.

## Reproduce
From the repo root, with `data/base/` in place (`base-grid-v2`):
```
python src/viz3d/build_terrain_assets.py      # writes data/tiles/3d/ (about 4 s)
python -m pytest tests/viz3d -q
node --test tests/viz3d/                      # camera maths, plan model, rehearsal model, Mars time and Sun, shadow camera, view link, photos model, walking model, map layers model (Node 26; nothing to install)
python src/viz3d/paper_figures.py             # paper/figures/p1_fig1_relief_exaggeration.jpg, p1_fig2_sun_sol4711.png and .json (needs node)
node src/viz3d/sun_table.mjs --sol 4711 --x 8143537.49 --y -284463.08      # the Sun through a sol, as JSON
python -m uvicorn api.main:app --port 8000    # then open http://localhost:8000/3d/
```
Checks in the page: `/3d/?check=shading`, `/3d/?check=route`, `/3d/?check=rehearse`, `/3d/?check=sun`, `/3d/?check=shadows`, `/3d/?check=guide`, `/3d/?check=walk`, `/3d/?check=layers`, and P3's `/3d/?check=photos` and `/3d/?check=stations` (`paper/notes/P3_rover_photos.md`); add `&q=low` for the low preset; each shows its result as JSON. The shadows check takes about 12 s (7 s in the low preset) and keeps the page busy meanwhile; the layers check takes about 8 s.
Speed: `/3d/?q=low&fps=1&spin=1` (the readout shows frames per second); views: "Copy link to this view" in the About section. How to use the page: `web/3d/README.md`.
The tour fixture is in git; it was made with `git archive origin/9-e5-orienteering src/orienteering | tar -x -C <scratch>` and `python src/viz3d/make_tour_fixture.py --e5-src <scratch>/src` (about 20 s; it plans in-process and saves nothing).
The fixture of real local times is in git; it was made with `python src/viz3d/make_lmst_fixture.py --index-dir <raw data>/03_curiosity/imagenes_crudas/indice_soles_4450_4848` (the raw-image index is not in the repo).
`MARTIAN_DATA` selects another data folder, as in `api/main.py`. **`data/tiles/3d/` is not in the `tiles-v2` release:** if `data/tiles/` is downloaded or unzipped again, run the first command again. Without it the page shows that command.
