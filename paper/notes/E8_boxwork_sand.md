# E8 · Sand and boxwork ridges from the 0.25 m orthophoto (weak labels + U-Net)

Owner: JB (`@JavierBonilla2004`) · Issue: #11 · PRs: #49 · Release: `e8-v0` · Status: v0 — sand usable (conservative), ridges a negative result · Last update: 2026-11-14

## Question
Inside the boxwork, the DEM features do not explain where Curiosity drove (E2: AUC ≈ 0.5). Can the 0.25 m HiRISE orthophoto add what the DEM misses — where the loose sand and the bright boxwork ridges are — as per-cell layers on the base grid for the cost (E1, E3), the walkback (E4) and the stations (E5)?

## Data
- **Orthophoto:** `ortho_color_025.tif` (HiRISE colour, 8000 × 8000 px at 0.25 m) [HiRISE-Gale], cropped to the contract bbox (base-grid note). Detail from 24 HiRISE images acquired 2006–2011 (PSP_001488 to ESP_025368), before landing; LRGB colour fused onto it in 2025.
- **DEM layers** for comparison: `slope_deg`, `roughness`, `tpi`, `residual` (base grid, 1 m) [Parker2016].
- **No pixel-level reference exists** for the Gale boxwork: published mappings are manual and not released as GIS files [Siebach2014]. Training labels were therefore generated by rules (weak labels), and the evaluation used blind hand labels and Curiosity's route.
- **Route:** PLACES `localized_interp.csv`, `ROVER` poses [PLACES]; boxwork sols 4541–4848 (provisional).

## Method

### Spatial split (decided before any tuning)
The bbox is split into quadrants of 1 × 1 km. **Validation = SW-S**, the southern half of the SW quadrant (0.5 × 1 km), where Curiosity's boxwork campaign zigzags. **Training = NW, NE, SE and SW-N.** All rule thresholds are percentiles of the training regions only; training patches keep a 16 m buffer from SW-S; previews never showed SW-S labels or predictions. The first choice was the whole SW quadrant; it was narrowed to SW-S after training-only previews showed that most of the polygonal network lies in SW, so the training set would otherwise contain almost no boxwork. No validation labels had been seen at that point.

### Weak labels (`src/dl_boxwork/weak_labels.py`, `weak_labels_v3.py`)
Classes: 0 background, 1 ridge, 2 sand/trough, 255 ignore.
- **v1 (negative):** white/black grey top-hat on luminance (6.25 m window). Sand (black top-hat + grey-blue colour) worked; "ridges" fired on any small bright object, mostly loose clasts.
- **v2 (negative):** Sato ridge filter [Sato1998] at 0.5 m (scales 0.5–1.5 m) with fragments < 10–20 m² removed. Clasts were rejected, but bright layer edges, ledges and dense rubble were still labelled as ridges.
- **v3 (used):** context instead of local shape. (1) Sand = grey-blue and darker than the median (blueness ≥ p75 of training). (2) Watershed of the smoothed luminance flooded from sand troughs; divide lines that agree with a Sato response at ridge scale (1.5–3 m) are candidate ridges. (3) Regional boxwork score S (Gaussian window σ = 17.5 m), 0–3: low double-angle orientation coherence R2 < 0.5 (parallel layers give R2 ≈ 1), compact watershed cells (50–600 m², aspect < 3), local sand share 15–60 %. (4) Ridge only where S ≥ 2; bright structures where S = 0 are labelled background (to teach that layer edges and rubble are not boxwork); 0.5 m ignore band on class boundaries.

### U-Net (`make_patches.py`, `train_unet.py`, `predict_unet.py`)
- 256 × 256 px patches (64 m): 748 training, 105 validation.
- U-Net, EfficientNet-B0 encoder pre-trained on ImageNet [Ronneberger2015; Tan2019], 3 input channels (colour: sand is recognised by its grey-blue tone), segmentation_models_pytorch.
- Loss: class-weighted cross-entropy (weights 1 : 10 : 6.16 for background : ridge : sand, inverse frequency capped at 10) + Dice, ignore index 255. Patches with ridges or sand are oversampled (weights 5 and 3). Flips and 90° rotations; AdamW 3·10⁻⁴, cosine schedule, batch 8, 15 epochs.
- **Float32 training.** With mixed precision, one batch per epoch overflowed to NaN; it was skipped in the loss but had already updated the BatchNorm running statistics (64 layers with NaN), so evaluation predicted background everywhere. Float32 fixed it (and was faster on the T1000: 20 s per epoch).
- Model selection: epoch with the best mean IoU of ridge and sand against the weak labels of SW-S (a proxy, not the evaluation).
- Prediction over the whole bbox with 1024 px windows (128 px overlap, averaged softmax); `argmax` classes at 0.25 m, then **`sand.tif` / `ridge.tif` = fraction of the 4 × 4 sub-cells of each 1 m cell** predicted as that class.

### Evaluation 1: blind points (`point_labels.py`)
Stratified random sampling by predicted class in SW-S (80 points per class, 240 in total) with area-weighted estimators [Olofsson2014]. The labeller sees only the orthophoto and a cross-hair. Two passes on the same points: pass 1, then pass 2 with a strict sand rule ("dark, smooth, continuous surface with no visible clasts; a clast mosaic with grey in between is background"). An earlier attempt with painted patches was dropped: it was slow and did not include background.

### Evaluation 2: Curiosity's route (`eval_with_rover.py`)
Same corridor design as E2: cells driven by the rover (route densified every 0.25 m, widened to 3 m) against alternative cells 2–50 m away, in 5 route blocks of equal size. AUC_avoid = probability that a driven cell has a lower value than an alternative cell (0.5 = no information).

## Results

### Weak labels v3 (training regions)
Source: `data/e8/weak_labels_summary.json`. Background 28.5 %, ridge 1.2 %, sand 4.6 %, ignore 65.6 % of pixels. S ≥ 2 covers 20.6 % of the training area, concentrated in the central band of dark troughs. Ridge cover inside those regions is 15.4 % (literature: 20–50 % [Siebach2014]); median compact cell 108.5 m² (literature: cells of 10–20 m).

### Training and map
Source: `data/e8/train_log.json`, `predict_summary.json`. Best epoch 3 (IoU against weak labels in SW-S: sand 0.876, ridge 0.695, background 0.986); agreement with the weak labels then declined slowly while the training loss kept falling, as expected when a network starts fitting label noise. Predicted map: background 86.2 %, ridge 5.5 %, sand 8.2 % of the bbox. Figures: [e8_fig1_training.png](../figures/e8_fig1_training.png), [e8_fig2_sand_map.png](../figures/e8_fig2_sand_map.png).

Agreement between the two passes: **71.2 %** of 240 points, almost all of the disagreement in the ridge class (27 ridge answers in pass 1, 1 in pass 2). Figure: [e8_fig3_point_validation.png](../figures/e8_fig3_point_validation.png).

**Reading:** when the map says sand, it is sand about 92 % of the time, stable across both passes; but it finds only about 1 in 9 sand pixels, so `sand.tif` is a conservative lower bound. Predicted ridges are not reliable, and ridges were not identified consistently by the (non-expert) labeller either.

### Curiosity's route
Source: `data/e8/rover_eval.json`. 13,641 driven cells, 308,623 alternative cells.

| Layer | AUC_avoid, whole route | Boxwork sols | SW-S (held out) | Mean value, driven vs alternatives (boxwork) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **sand (E8)** | 0.515 (blocks 0.509–0.529) | **0.523** (3 blocks 0.516–0.529) | 0.505 | **0.039 vs 0.085** |
| ridge (E8) | 0.496 | 0.500 | 0.506 | 0.146 vs 0.155 |
| slope_deg (DEM) | 0.502 | 0.467 | 0.466 | 12.7° vs 11.5° |
| roughness (DEM) | 0.496 | 0.450 | 0.462 | 0.288 vs 0.261 m |

The rover drove over less than half the predicted sand of its alternatives, in every block; the AUC stays close to 0.5 because most cells have sand = 0 (ties). For comparison, E2's logistic model on all DEM features reached AUC 0.526 in the 50 m corridor. Inside the boxwork the rover took slightly *steeper and rougher* cells than its alternatives (AUC 0.45–0.47): it avoided sand and stayed on rock. Predicted sand patches are small (median 4.4 m², p90 39.1 m², n = 4801), consistent with the low producer's accuracy. Figure: [e8_fig4_rover_auc.png](../figures/e8_fig4_rover_auc.png).

### Use downstream (E1, E2)
From Juan's E1 (PR #57): with start and goal given, a sand-only cost beats distance on unseen route blocks (4 of 5) with a stable positive price, and the driven route crosses about 8 % less sand than the model's routes. It is the first layer that helps out of sample; the DEM layers do not. Per cell (E2 logistic, 50 m corridor) adding sand only moves the AUC from 0.526 to 0.531 (4 of 5 blocks), within noise, because most cells have sand = 0; over the whole box, 0.730 to 0.726.

### v1 attempt: can a threshold recover the missed sand? (issue #93)
Source: `data/e8/sand_calibration.json`, `missed_sand_gallery.png` (`calibrate_sand.py`, `missed_sand_gallery.py`). The sand probability was saved (`predict_unet.py --prob-only`) and the rule "sand if p_sand >= t" was scored on the 240 blind points with the same area-weighted estimators; t was chosen on one random half and scored on the other (200 repeated 2-fold splits). Even at t = 0.05, out-of-sample recall only moves from 0.110 to **0.115** (95 % band 0.087–0.161; precision 0.916). At the 39 missed sand points p_sand has median **0.004** (8 % above 0.05): the network is not uncertain there, it is confidently wrong, an error inherited from the weak labels (sand = grey-blue). Figure: [e8_fig5_threshold.png](../figures/e8_fig5_threshold.png).

The missed sand looks different ([e8_fig6_missed_sand.png](../figures/e8_fig6_missed_sand.png)): reddish-brown, smooth or mixed with gravel, without the grey-blue tone. It is likely dust-covered sand, which at 0.25 m resembles smooth bedrock. The labeller was not consistent on these points either: only **22 of 39** were also called sand in pass 1 (56 %). A confident bias cannot be fixed by uncertainty methods (MC dropout, CVaR costs, uncertainty sampling), and new labels of this class would be unreliable, so no v1 was trained (negative result).

**Post-hoc reading (not the original design):** `sand.tif` is a high-precision map of dark grey-blue sand — likely loose, active basaltic sand, the kind Curiosity avoided (see Curiosity's route above). Dust-covered sand is not mapped.

### Does the DEM see the ridges?
At the pass-1 hand points (27 ridge, 118 sand, 95 background), `tpi` is +0.12 m on ridges and −0.07 m on sand (AUC ridge > sand 0.66), `residual` +0.10 vs −0.08 m (0.68); `slope_deg` and `roughness` show nothing (0.49). The DEM keeps a weak height signal of the walls but not their shape. Used as a filter on predicted ridges (TPI > 0), precision only rose from 15 % to 22.5 %, so it does not rescue the ridge class.

## Checks
- Training patches never overlap SW-S or its 16 m buffer (checked in `make_patches.py`); rule thresholds come from training regions only.
- No NaN in the saved model (all BatchNorm statistics finite) after float32 training.
- Point sampling is stratified and shuffled; the labeller never saw predictions or weak labels in SW-S.
- Scripts were tested on synthetic images with known answers (polygonal grid vs parallel layers for v3; known avoidance for the route AUC).

## Limitations
- **One non-expert labeller**, two passes, 240 points. Sand definition was ambiguous in pass 1 (clast mosaics); pass 2 fixed the rule. A second labeller would measure inter-annotator agreement.
- **The network inherits the rules' errors**: it never saw a hand label during training.
- Sand recall is low (~11 %) and lowering the threshold does not raise it (v1 attempt, #93): `sand.tif` maps dark grey-blue sand only; dust-covered sand is missed.
- Rover-route AUCs measure usefulness, not correctness; drivers also chose targets for science and operations, and saw the ground at higher resolution.
- **Image date:** the orthophoto detail comes from 24 HiRISE images acquired 2006–2011 (PSP_001488 to ESP_025368), all before landing and about 13 years before the boxwork campaign (2025), so it shows no rover tracks [HiRISE-Gale]. Small sand patches may have moved since, which adds noise but cannot create the measured avoidance. The LRGB colour was fused onto it in 2025 from HiRISE colour strips whose dates are not listed; the U-Net relies on colour for sand.
- The validation region was narrowed from SW to SW-S after training-only previews (no validation labels seen).
- Boxwork sol range is provisional; ridge widths and heights in the literature vary (1–5 m wide, 0.2–2 m high).

## What other pieces can use
- **E1 (Juan):** `sand.tif` as a cost layer (already in `main`, PR #57; see Results).
- **E3 (Sebas):** `sand.tif` as a terrain factor (loose sand costs much more per metre than firm ground in Pandolf-type models); treat it as a lower bound.
- **E5 (Diana):** stations from sand-rock contacts (`stations_e8.gpkg`, next block), because ridges are not reliable.
- **Do not use `ridge.tif`.**

## Reproduce
From `data/`, with `base-grid-v2`, PLACES and the `martian` environment (GPU optional):
```bash
python ../src/dl_boxwork/weak_labels_v3.py --val SW-S --blue-p 75 --ridge-p 75
python ../src/dl_boxwork/make_patches.py --val SW-S
python ../src/dl_boxwork/train_unet.py --epochs 15 --no-amp
python ../src/dl_boxwork/predict_unet.py
python ../src/dl_boxwork/point_labels.py score      # needs e8/points.csv (release e8-v0)
python ../src/dl_boxwork/eval_with_rover.py
python ../src/dl_boxwork/paper_figures_e8.py
```
