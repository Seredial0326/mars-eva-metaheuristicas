Gale crater, Mars

Every step keeps a way home.

Mars EVA Planner plans an astronaut's walk through the boxwork of Gale crater and checks, at every point, that there is enough energy and time to get back. Then you rehearse it in 3D and follow it on an arm tablet.

Follow the demo step by step

1Plan

Choose stations. It finds the walk.

Set the habitat, the time and the energy, and the planner picks which science stations to visit and in what order. With 13 stations and 120 minutes, it visits 8 and keeps a way back from every point.

Plan (2D) 2D planner user guide

2Check the way back

The red line follows the ground.

The red line marks how far the astronaut can go and still be home within the 30 minutes of emergency oxygen. Scarps and slopes shape it, so it is never a circle.

3Rehearse

Walk it first, at eye height.

The 3D view lays the plan on the HiRISE terrain, under the Sun of the same sol, with the same meters. Curiosity's 383 Navcam photos sit where they were taken.

Open in 3D 3D map user guide

4Follow

It answers on site, without Earth.

A radio signal takes 3–22 minutes each way between Mars and Earth, so the arm tablet works alone. It speaks the next turn, shows energy, time back and radio, and turns home in a solar storm.

What it does not claim

  • It is tested in simulation and against Curiosity's route, not with astronauts.
  • The energy model is an extrapolation to a suited astronaut on Mars.
  • There is no GPS on Mars, so the walk on the tablet is simulated.

The paper

The science behind it.

Planning astronaut EVAs in the Gale crater boxwork: terrain-aware walkback, station tours and a learned terrain price, tested against Curiosity's traverse

Mars EVA Planner team · NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · draft

We price every step of a suited walk on a 1 m elevation model under Mars gravity, check the way home from every point, and choose the science stations with an exact solver. Then we test the terrain model against the route Curiosity actually drove.

  • Simple rules call 21–46 % of their goals safe when their own route cannot complete them. The planner never does.
  • About a third of the area inside a fixed 1.35 km radius cannot be left and re-entered within the 30 min emergency oxygen reserve.
  • The terrain explains which valley Curiosity chose, not its path within the boxwork. Only sand earns a small learned price.
  • An adaptive reserve cuts oxygen exhaustion from 90 % to 4.7 % of simulated walks when the astronaut uses 1.5 times the predicted energy.
