Mars EVA Planner · 3D map · user guide

Walk the plan before anyone leaves the habitat

The 3D map shows the walk you planned in the 2D planner on the real shape of the ground, lit by the Sun of Mars. You can play the walk at eye level with the same meters as the astronaut's arm tablet, and practise what to do when something goes wrong.

How to read this guide: each orange number on a screenshot points at a button or a reading. The same number in the list below the picture says what it does. Point at a list item to light up its spot on the picture.

Open the 3D map

The 3D map shows the same 2 × 2 km area of Gale crater as the 2D planner: a terrain model with 1 m detail, covered by a HiRISE orbital photo with 25 cm detail. It does not make plans: it shows the plan you saved in the 2D planner. It runs on your computer and needs no internet.

The easiest way in: plan a walk in the 2D planner, then use the 3D link in its step 4 (Walk). To start it yourself:

python src/viz3d/build_terrain_assets.py      # only the first time: builds the 3D terrain
python -m uvicorn api.main:app --port 8000    # then open http://localhost:8000/3d/

If no plan has been saved yet, the ground still shows, and the panel offers Plan with default settings.

Plan with default settings replaces your saved plan. The 2D planner and the tablet read that same plan, so use this button only when there is no plan you want to keep.

The screen

The ground fills the screen. The panel on the left holds the plan and the settings, in sections that open and close. At the start only Plan is open (on a small window, all of them start closed). The route has two colours: cyan on the way out, pink on the way back.

  1. 1
    Panel

    Sections: Plan, Sun, Map layer, Rover photos and About this view. Click a title to open or close it.

  2. 2
    Local relief

    ×1 shows the real height of the ground. ×1.5 to ×5 make small hills and slopes look taller, so they are easier to see. It starts at ×2 (“Local relief ×2 (exaggerated)”).

  3. 3
    Plan

    The plan in short: distance, time, energy, the stations visited and the ones left out.

  4. 4
    ← Planner

    Goes back to the 2D planner, to change the plan.

  5. 5
    Compass

    The needle points north. Click it to face north.

  6. 6
    Frame

    Goes back to the starting view of the whole area.

  7. 7
    Habitat and stations

    Pins on the ground. Stations the plan does not visit look faded. Click a pin to open its card.

  8. 8
    Position

    Map position and height of the point at the centre of the screen, in metres. While you rehearse or walk, it shows where the walker is.

Read the plan

The Plan section repeats what the 2D planner decided, in plain words, and offers two ways to go through it: Rehearse it at eye level, or Walk freely.

  1. 1
    Where it comes from

    “Last plan from the planner”: the plan you saved in the 2D planner.

  2. 2
    Distance, time, energy

    For example “1392 m · 111 min · 448 Wh of 900 usable”.

  3. 3
    Route colours

    Cyan on the way out, pink on the way back.

  4. 4
    What the planner says

    The stations visited and in what order, the ones left out, and the energy used at the stations. An amber ⚠ marks only what is worth checking, such as a habitat the planner had to move.

  5. 5
    Stations (13)

    The full list: first the visited stations in order, then the others.

  6. 6
    A station in the list

    Click it, or press Enter, to fly there and open its card.

  7. 7
    Reload plan

    Loads the plan again after you change it in the 2D planner.

  8. 8
    Rehearse

    Plays the walk at eye level, with a clock and the tablet meters. More below.

  9. 9
    Walk freely

    Walk anywhere, like in a game. Works without a plan. More below.

A station's card

  1. 1
    Choose a station

    From the list, or click its pin on the ground.

  2. 2
    The card

    What kind of station it is, its science score, the time needed there and its place in the order. For R3: Boxwork ridge, score 5, 10 min, first. If a rover photo was taken within 150 m, the card shows it too.

  3. 3
    Fly here

    Moves the view close to the station, about 60 m away.

  4. 4
    Close

    Click × to close the card. During the rehearsal, Esc closes it too.

Rehearse the walk

This is the main use of the 3D map. Rehearse plays the planned walk at eye level, on the walk's own clock, with the same instructions and meters as the astronaut's arm tablet. The panel hides so you can see the ground, the relief goes back to its real height (×1), and the Sun moves with the clock.

  1. 1
    Next instruction

    As on the tablet: an arrow, the distance in large type (“220 m”) and what to do (“Arrive at station R3”). With Voice on, it is read aloud.

  2. 2
    Play / Pause

    Or Space, when no button or field is selected.

  3. 3
    Return home

    Stops the walk and takes the fastest way home from here, drawn in red.

  4. 4
    Off route

    Practice: the walker strays 25 m from the route and the guide brings them back.

  5. 5
    Solar storm

    Practice: a storm alert and the automatic way home, with the radiation dose.

  6. 6
    More

    Chase view, Recentre, speed, Voice and an energy test.

  7. 7
    Panel

    Shows or hides the side panel without stopping.

  8. 8
    Exit

    Ends the rehearsal. Esc does not end it: it only closes a card and pauses.

  9. 9
    Timeline

    Drag it to jump to any moment of the walk.

  10. 10
    Walk clock

    Time, leg and distance so far: “EVA 6:01 of 111:27 · outbound · 262 of 1392 m”.

  11. 11
    Sun line

    Solar time and Sun height at this moment, and the time the walk started (11:00, the best time to leave).

  12. 12
    Energy bar

    Energy used, energy needed to get home, and energy to spare, as in the 2D planner.

  13. 13
    Tablet meters

    Spare energy, Home in (limit 30 min), Radio, Spare energy at your pace and EVA time left. Each one, except Radio, shows a word, OK LOW or TURN BACK, and a bar in that colour. Radio says how the link goes: “Direct to the habitat”, “Relay through your partner” or “No radio link”. Spare energy at your pace appears after the first 30 m.

  14. 14
    Return home from here

    When paused: the time and energy to walk home from this point. On the ground, the red line is 30 min from home, and the cyan area is what the energy left can still reach and come back from.

The More menu

  1. 1
    Chase view

    See the astronaut from behind.

  2. 2
    Recentre

    Looks ahead again after you drag to look around. A double-click does the same.

  3. 3
    Speed

    ×1 (real time), ×30, ×60 or ×180. It starts at ×60.

  4. 4
    Voice

    Reads the instructions aloud. It starts on.

  5. 5
    Use ×k

    Test: makes the astronaut use more or less energy than planned. Each click changes it: 1.0 → 1.2 → 1.5 → 0.8.

  6. 6
    Energy numbers

    The plan's estimate at this point: energy used, needed to get home and to spare, the oxygen used, and the time home.

At each station

The walk stops at every station and shows its card. The clock counts the time spent working there.

  1. 1
    Station card

    When you arrive (“11 min into the EVA”), how long to stay, why the station matters and what to do there.

  2. 2
    Done, continue

    Carries on walking.

  3. 3
    Return home from here

    Whether going home now still fits: “✓ 10 min (limit 30) · ✓ 53 Wh, 783 Wh spare after it”.

  4. 4
    The next leg

    “50 m · Arrive at station R2”.

If the astronaut tires faster than planned

As the walk goes on, the tablet compares the energy the astronaut really uses with the plan's estimate, and recalculates the energy to spare at that real pace. To see it, open More and click Use ×k until it reads ×1.5.

  1. 1
    Use ×1.5

    The test astronaut now uses 50 % more energy than planned.

  2. 2
    Spare energy at your pace

    The meter that uses the astronaut's real pace instead of the plan's estimate.

  3. 3
    Measured pace

    “k 1.06 (90 % 1.00–1.12) · 634 Wh”. k says how many times more energy than planned is being used; the brackets give its likely range, and the last number is the energy to spare at that pace. k moves towards 1.5 as more of the walk is seen.

  4. 4
    Energy numbers

    Still the plan's estimate. The extra energy of the test astronaut shows in the meter above and in the final summary.

If going home no longer fits at the real pace, a RETURN NOW alert appears (screen readers announce it). In the test for this guide (900 Wh, ×1.5) it did not appear; with a smaller energy budget it does.

Practise what can go wrong

Three buttons in the rehearsal practise an emergency: Return home, Off route and Solar storm. The way home is drawn in red and the way back to the route in orange. The walk clock keeps running through them, and the new line stays until you press Exit or Replay.

Solar storm

  1. 1
    Storm banner

    “SOLAR STORM · return home now”, as on the tablet, with a red edge around the view.

  2. 2
    Solar storm

    The button that starts the practice storm.

  3. 3
    Heading home

    The instruction now leads to the habitat.

  4. 4
    Clock of the way home

    “EVA 11:30 · return home 2:30 of 8:23 · 112 of 380 m”. Why and how much shows the radiation dose.

Off route

  1. 1
    Off route

    The walker steps 25 m away from the route.

  2. 2
    Back to the route

    The next instruction steers the walker back to the route.

  3. 3
    New end time

    The clock adds the way back to the route, drawn in orange.

Back at the habitat

  1. 1
    Plan against the walk

    What the rehearsal did next to what the plan said: time 111 min (plan 111), energy 447 Wh (448), distance 1363 m (1392), stations 8 (8), metres without direct radio, how far it strayed, and k.

  2. 2
    Summary

    Opens the summary again after you close it.

  3. 3
    Replay

    Starts again from the beginning, as a new walk with its own summary. Space does the same.

  4. 4
    Exit

    Back to the normal view.

Sun and shadows

The scene is lit by the Sun of Mars on a chosen day and hour. Once a plan is shown, the Sun moves to that plan's best time to leave: 11:00 for this 111 min plan (limit 120 min) on sol 4711, the same time the 2D planner gives. Late in the afternoon, long shadows show which slopes the walk crosses in the shade.

The orbital photo has shadows of its own, because it was taken in the middle of the afternoon. To see only the light of the hour you chose, turn on Relief only.

  1. 1
    Curiosity sol

    The Mars day, counted from Curiosity's landing. It starts at 4711.

  2. 2
    Local solar time

    Drag to move the Sun across the day. The picture shows 17:30.

  3. 3
    Where the light comes from

    For example “Sun 7° high, light from the west”, with the date on Earth. Point at it for the exact numbers.

  4. 4
    Note

    Tells you when the Sun was set to the best time to leave.

  5. 5
    Relief only

    Hides the photo: only the shape of the ground, lit by this Sun.

  6. 6
    Shadows

    Shows the shadows the hills cast. They are true to scale only at relief ×1.

Map layer

Lays one map of the 2D planner over the ground, with the same colours and legend: Radio coverage, Slope, Too steep to cross, Sand detected, Walking cost or Boulders. It is off at the start, and it stays on while you rehearse.

  1. 1
    Layer

    Choose one, or Off. Radio coverage is measured from the plan's habitat; Too steep to cross uses the plan's slope limit.

  2. 2
    Opacity

    How solid the layer looks: the higher, the less of the photo shows through.

  3. 3
    Legend

    Same scale as in the 2D planner (for slope: 0°, 15°, 30°, 45° and more).

Walk freely

Walk anywhere in the area, seen through the astronaut's eyes. You start next to the habitat, facing the way the route leaves. The route becomes a path on the ground in the colours of its legs, and each station a ring with a flag.

  1. 1
    Walk bar

    The buttons, and a reminder of the keys.

  2. 2
    Run

    3 m/s. Or hold Shift.

  3. 3
    Fast

    Travel at 20 m/s. Or press F.

  4. 4
    Chase view

    See the astronaut from behind. Or press C.

  5. 5
    Exit

    Or Esc. If the mouse is captured, the first Esc only frees it.

  6. 6
    Keys

    W/S or ↑/↓ forward and back, A/D sideways, ←/→ turn.

  7. 7
    Speed and route

    “Walking 1.0 m/s · on the route”, or how far away the route is.

Walking freely is a game, not the real walk. You can go through rocks and up any slope at the same speed: the planner's slope limit and energy cost do not apply. To check the plan, use Rehearse.

Rover photos

Photos taken by Curiosity's navigation camera (Navcam), placed where the camera stood. In the Rover photos section, Show photo stops puts orange camera pins on the ground. They are off at the start, and hidden while you rehearse or walk freely. A pin opens the stop's card: the photo, one button per view, the date, sol, local time, direction and credit.

View from here puts you where the camera was, at real relief (×1) and with the Sun of the day and hour of the photo, and lays the photo over the view. The slider fades the photo in and out, Previous and Next change the view, Realign lines the view up again after you drag it, and Exit (or Esc) puts everything back. A station's card shows the nearest photo within 150 m, with View the rover photo.

The photos need the photo catalogue in data/tiles/3d/photos/. Without it, the section says “No rover photos: the server has no photo catalogue …”, and everything else still works.

Share a view

The About this view section saves or shares what you are looking at.

  1. 1
    Copy link to this view

    A web address that opens this same view: camera, relief, sol, hour and shadows. On another computer of the network, the link appears in a box to copy by hand.

  2. 2
    Save image

    Saves the ground, the route and the pins as a PNG picture, without the panels.

At the bottom of the section, Quality says how detailed the ground is (“Quality: high · mesh every 2 m · …”). On a slow computer, add ?q=low to the address (a point every 4 m). Touch screens, small screens and computers with little memory start on low.

Six short exercises

Start with a plan from the 2D planner, for example 120 min and 1,000 Wh with the 13 saved stations.

3 MIN

Read the ground

  1. Move Local relief to ×4 and turn the view.
  2. Map layer: Slope, then Too steep to cross.

You see the small hills and drops the route goes around.

5 MIN

Rehearse the plan

  1. Rehearse, then Play at ×60.
  2. At R3, read the card, then Done, continue.
  3. Pause anywhere and read Return home from here.

You see the red 30 min line and the cyan safe area around the walker.

3 MIN

Light at departure

  1. Sun: drag the hour from 07:00 to 17:30 with Shadows on.
  2. Go back to the best time to leave.

You see which slopes are in the shade at each hour.

3 MIN

Something goes wrong

  1. Rehearse; after a few minutes press Solar storm.
  2. Replay, try Off route, then Return home.

You see the red way home, the orange way back to the route, and the clock that keeps running.

4 MIN

A tired astronaut

  1. Rehearse, then More → Use ×k until it reads ×1.5.
  2. Play and watch Spare energy at your pace.

You see k climb from 1 towards 1.5 and the spare energy drop.

3 MIN

Walk the ridges

  1. Walk freely, then press C for the chase view.
  2. Follow the path to R3, then press F to travel fast.

You see how big the ground is from the astronaut's eyes.

What the 3D map can tell you, and what it cannot

On screenWhat it isWhat it is not
ReliefThe terrain model with 1 m detail: the hillside, small hills and dropsThe boxwork ridges, 1–2 m high, which only the photo shows. Above ×1 the height is exaggerated
ShadowsShadows of the relief, from the real position of the Sun of MarsRight when the relief is above ×1 (they are true only at ×1), or real brightness: no dust or air is simulated
The rehearsalThe plan played back on its own clock, with the tablet's rulesA new plan: it never changes the route
MetersThe arm tablet's: energy, time to get home, radio, energy at your pace, walk time leftMeasurements: the energy comes from a model, and the extra effort is simulated with Use ×k
Walk freelyA way to see the ground at eye levelThe real walk: no slope limit and no energy cost
Rover photosWhere the camera stood and where it lookedProof that a station can be seen from there: the ground may hide it

Address options

Add these to the address (http://localhost:8000/3d/?q=low; join several with &).

AddWhat it does
?q=low · ?q=highLess or more detail in the ground
?k=3Starting relief height, from 1 to 5
?sol=4711&lt=15:00The day (sol) and hour of the Sun
?shadows=0Start without shadows
?drag=panWeb-map mouse: drag slides, right-drag turns
?photo=<id>Open a rover photo in the photo view
?plan=defaultMake a plan with the default settings when the page opens. Replaces your saved plan.
