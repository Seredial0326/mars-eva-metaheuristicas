Plan a walk on Mars you can come back from, checked at every step

The user guide of the Mars EVA Planner's 2D planner. It shows every button on real screens. Each orange number points at a control; the same number in the list below the picture says what it does. Hover a list item, or move to it with the keyboard, to light up its control.

What the planner does, and how to open it

You pick science stations inside a 2 × 2 km area of the boxwork in Gale crater, where Curiosity worked in 2025–2026, and you set what the EVA may spend: time, energy, a reserve and, if you want, oxygen. The planner returns the walk that collects the most science value within those limits, in the best order, and it checks one thing at every point of the route: the energy already used plus the energy needed to walk back to the habitat fits the budget. That rule is called walkback. The check is exact on the model's costs (walking energy on the 1 m terrain model, oxygen estimated from that energy); a body that works harder than the model is a separate risk, covered by the tablet's adaptive meter. Cells steeper than the slope limit are never crossed.

Everything runs on a local server. No internet connection is needed while you plan or walk.

python -m pip install -r api/requirements.txt     # once
python -m api.check_data                          # says which data are missing and how to get them
python -m uvicorn api.main:app --port 8000        # then open http://localhost:8000/

The screen

On the left, the panel with the steps; on the right, the map of the area. Work through the tabs from left to right. Each tab ends with a Next button that takes you to the following one.

  1. 1
    Tabs

    1 Terrain, 2 Stations, 3 Plan, 4 Walk, and Context. A check mark on Plan means there is a plan; a dot means it is out of date.

  2. 2
    Panel of the current step

    Everything you set for that step. Long explanations are folded under More.

  3. 3
    Map

    Drag to move, scroll to zoom. Stations, the habitat, the route and the safety lines are drawn here.

  4. 4
    On the map

    The legend lists only what is drawn right now. Click the title to fold it.

  5. 5
    Fit plan · Whole area

    Zoom to the planned walk, or back to the whole 2 × 2 km area.

  6. 6
    Cell readout

    Move the cursor over the map to read where it is: metres east and north of the area's south-west corner (hover the readout for the map coordinates and the cell).

  7. 7
    Data sources

    Which missions and data types feed the map. Opens the list in the Context tab.

Units. Positions are metres in the Mars map projection of the terrain model. Energy is in Wh, time in minutes, oxygen in litres, dose in µGy.

1 Terrain: read the ground first

Before choosing anything, look at where the ground is steep, where the sand is, and which cells nobody should cross. Switch layers on and off with their boxes; a slider under a layer sets its transparency or its limit.

  1. 1
    Photo from orbit

    HiRISE at 25 cm per pixel. The only layer that shows the boxwork ridges (bright lines) and the dark grey-blue sand.

  2. 2
    Shaded relief

    Light and shadow from the 1 m terrain model: knolls and scarps. It does not resolve the 1–3 m ridges.

  3. 3
    Slope

    Steepest slope of each cell: green 0°, yellow 15°, orange 30°, red 45° or more. The slider sets its transparency.

  4. 4
    Never cross cells steeper than N°

    The cells the planner will never step on, drawn as a dark hatch. Default 30°. This is the setting that changes routes the most.

  5. 5
    How much is blocked

    "12.4 % of the area is off limits" at 30°. At 20° about 30 % is blocked.

  6. 6
    Sand detected

    Share of each 1 m cell detected as sand. When it marks sand it is right about 92 % of the time, but it misses most of the sand.

  7. 7
    Boulders

    Dots on 10 m squares: the bigger the dot, the more boulder cover. Boulders larger than about 1 m. Shown for context; the route cost does not use them.

  8. 8
    Walking cost

    Wh to cross each 1 m cell. The cheapest metre is a gentle descent, not flat ground.

  9. 9
    Radio coverage

    Cells from which a suit radio sees the habitat antenna, and the stretches of the route with no direct radio. Line of sight on the terrain model: cautious.

  10. 10
    Night temperature

    Mars Odyssey night infrared at 81 m. Context only.

  11. 11
    Rover photos

    Where Curiosity's Navcam photos were taken and where each one looks. Needs the photo catalogue on the server; without it the box says "Unavailable" and the rest works.

Changing the slope limit marks an existing plan as out of date. Plan again to see the new route.

2 Stations: mark where the science is

A station is a place worth visiting. Each one has a type, a work time (minutes of service at the station) and a science value. The planner visits as much value as fits in the limits.

  1. 1
    Type

    Boxwork ridge, Hollow between ridges, Contact between units, or Curiosity measurement site. New stations take this type.

  2. 2
    ＋ Add stations by clicking the map

    Turns on placing: every click on the map adds a station of the chosen type.

  3. 3
    Load Curiosity sites

    Adds the places where Curiosity drilled.

  4. 4
    Advanced

    Folded: the science values and the sites found by the boxwork detector.

  5. 5
    Load AI-found sites

    Adds sand–rock contacts found on the orbital photo. They are added unsaved.

  6. 6
    Science value by type

    Points per type: ridge 5, hollow 4, contact 4, Curiosity site 3. Edit them and the next plan follows.

  7. 7
    Stations table

    Id, type, work time and value of every station; change them in place or remove a row with ×. A "?" after an id marks a rough position (some of the loaded Curiosity sites).

  8. 8
    Save stations

    Keeps the list for next time. Plans already use the list without saving.

Placing on the map

While a placing mode is on, a banner on the map says what a click will do. Esc stops it. Without a mouse: Tab to the map, move the cross-hair with the arrow keys (Shift for bigger steps) and press Enter.

  1. 1
    The mode is on

    The button stays pressed while placing.

  2. 2
    Banner

Save rewrites the whole list. Saving stores exactly what is loaded now, values included. If you loaded the AI-found sites or edited values for a test, reload the page before saving.

3 Plan: set the limits and get the walk

Say where the walk starts and what it may spend. The planner chooses which stations fit and in what order to visit them, and checks the way back at every point.

  1. 1
    Set on the map

    Click the map to move the habitat. A habitat on a blocked or cut-off cell is moved to the nearest valid one, and the plan says by how much.

  2. 2
    Where the habitat is

    "Centre of the area" by default. Back to the centre appears once you move it.

  3. 3
    EVA length

    Maximum duration in minutes, station work included.

  4. 4
    Energy budget

    Suit energy for the walk, from 50 to 2,800 Wh (a full suit is about 2,800).

  5. 5
    Reserve kept unused

    Part of the budget that is never planned. Default 10 %.

  6. 6
    Oxygen budget

    Under More options. Metabolic oxygen estimated from the energy; 0 means no limit.

  7. 7
    Charge extra energy on sand

    Uses the sand price learned from Curiosity's route, up to +11.5 % on sand.

  8. 8
    Plan the walk

    Or press Enter in any field. The first plan takes 10–30 s while the terrain graph is built; later ones are fast.

Reading the plan

Example: 120 min, 1,000 Wh, 10 % reserve, habitat at the centre, the 13 saved stations.

  1. 1
    Habitat and limits

    After planning, the inputs fold into one line. Click it to change them; the button now reads Plan again.

  2. 2
    The guarantee

    The astronaut can get back from every point: the walkback holds at every point of the route.

  3. 3
    What it spends

    Stations visited (8 of 13), time (111 of 120 min), energy (448 of 900 Wh after the reserve) and distance, each with a bar.

  4. 4
    Visiting order

    The stations in the order they are walked, and below them the ones left out because they do not fit.

  5. 5
    Radio

    "41 % of the walk without direct radio". Show on the map draws those stretches in magenta; More gives metres and minutes.

  6. 6
    Best time to leave

    Departures that keep the whole walk in sunlight (06:45–15:15 here); More gives the best one.

  7. 7
    Rehearse

    Opens step 4 and plays the walk.

  8. 8
    Fit plan

    Zooms the map to the walk. Numbers on the map give the visiting order.

Time runs out first. In this 2 × 2 km area a walk reaches its time limit long before its energy. Station work is most of the cost: on the full tour it is 61 % of the energy and 72 % of the time.

When nothing fits

The planner never invents a route. It says why there is no plan and what would fit. Example with a 60 Wh budget:

  1. 2
    Why, and what would fit

    "Not even the closest station, C1, fits on its own (energy: needs 57 Wh, 54 Wh available). To fit C1 alone you need an energy budget of at least 63 Wh." The previous plan stays on screen, marked Not updated.

Out of date. Change a limit, a station or the habitat after planning and the plan is marked OUT OF DATE until you plan again.

4 Walk: rehearse it and check safety

Play the walk and watch, at every moment, how long it takes to get home and how much energy is left. Pause anywhere to see how far the astronaut could still go.

  1. 1
    Home in N min · limit 30

    Time back to the habitat from where the walker is, against the 30 min of emergency oxygen.

  2. 2
    Spare energy

    Energy left after walking home from here. Below it: what returning costs on the plan.

  3. 3
    Margin to the 30 min line

    A word and a bar: OK TURN SOON OVER 30 MIN.

  4. 4
    Energy bar and clock

    Used, needed to return and spare; it only shrinks. The clock line reads "7:33 of 111:27 · used 41 Wh, returning needs 37 Wh", with oxygen and dose. Drag the slider to jump.

  5. 5
    Play / Pause

    Or Space. Here the walk plays through the stations; the tablet guide and the 3D rehearsal stop at each one.

  6. 6
    Speed

    ×30, ×60 or ×180.

  7. 7
    Tablet · 3D · Wrist card

    The same walk in the arm-tablet guide, in the 3D map, and as a printable step list.

  8. 8
    Show the safety lines

    Red line: the cells from which the astronaut can still walk home in 30 min. Dark shade: out of reach with the energy left. Neither is a circle.

  9. 9
    Compare with a fixed-distance rule

    Draws the classic 1.35 km and Apollo 1.7 km circles and says how much of them is not safe ("37 % of its 1.70 km circle cannot get back in 30 min").

  10. 10
    The walk on the map

    Route, walker, numbered stations and the safety lines.

Try another point

Place the astronaut anywhere, with any energy left, and see the way back from there.

  1. 3
    The answer for that point

    Time home and spare energy, computed from where you clicked ("At the point you placed, returning costs 18 Wh").

  2. 4
    The placed astronaut

    A magenta dot. The red line and the shade are redrawn for it. Set the energy with Energy still in the suit under Try another point.

Conditions around the walk

Radiation, weather and minerals inform the decision. None of them is part of the route cost.

  1. 1
    Radiation

    The normal dose rate measured by Curiosity (about 6.5 µGy per hour) and the dose over a sample walk. The background never changes the plan; a solar storm can.

  2. 2
    Simulate a solar storm

    Pick a storm in the list and press: the rehearsal pauses where the walker is, an alert shows the way home from there (the same time as the walk card) and its dose, and the amber line draws it. If the way home does not fit the energy left, the alert says so.

  3. 3
    Sol of the walk

    The Mars day to show: daylight hours, air and ground temperature, pressure and UV.

  4. 4
    Temperature through the campaign

    Daily minimum and maximum while Curiosity explored the boxwork; the dashed line is the chosen sol.

Further down: Minerals seen from orbit within 100 km (regional context) and Data sources, the list of every data type and mission.

Optional data. Radiation, weather, minerals, night temperature and photos need their data releases. If one is missing the panel says so (for example "E12 outputs missing in data/e12/") and everything else works. python -m api.check_data gives the download commands.

Learning routes

Short exercises that show what the planner can tell you. Start from a fresh page with the 13 saved stations.

5 MIN

Your first walk

  1. Terrain: turn on Slope and Sand detected.
  2. Plan: keep the defaults, set EVA length 120, press Plan the walk.
  3. Walk: press Play, pause halfway, press Show the safety lines.

You see: 8 of 13 stations, 111 min, and the red 30 min line around the walker.

3 MIN

Time is the limit

  1. Plan with EVA length 120, then 60 minutes.
  2. Compare the visiting order and the stations left out.

You see: with 60 min the highest-value ridges are kept. Energy is never the limit here.

3 MIN

The slope limit

  1. Terrain: move the limit from 30° to 20°.
  2. Plan again.

You see: about 30 % of the area blocked and a different route.

2 MIN

When nothing fits

  1. Plan: Energy budget 60 Wh, Plan again.
  2. Read why, then set 63 Wh and plan.

You see: the reason and the budget that fits; with 63 Wh only C1 is visited.

2 MIN

Fixed radius against the terrain

  1. Walk: Show the safety lines, tick Compare with a fixed-distance rule.
  2. Look at the cells inside the circle but outside the red line.

You see: about a third of what the circle calls safe cannot get back in 30 min.

2 MIN

Any point, any energy

  1. Walk: Try another point, place the astronaut far from the habitat.
  2. Lower the energy still in the suit.

You see: the dark shade grow as the energy falls.

2 MIN

Radio and light

  1. Plan: Show on the map, on the Radio line.
  2. Read Best time to leave.

You see: the magenta stretches with no direct radio, and the departure window in sunlight.

3 MIN

Science values

  1. Stations → Advanced: raise Hollow between ridges to 6.
  2. Plan with EVA length 60.

You see: which stations change when the value of a type changes.

What the numbers mean, and what they do not

NumberIt isIt is not
Energy (Wh)Metabolic energy from a walking model (Santee 2001, Mars gravity in the slope term, Apollo walking speed)A measurement of a suited astronaut on Mars; the model is an extrapolation
Oxygen (L)Estimated from the energy (20.1 kJ per litre)A tank reading
"Can get back from every point"Exact on the model and its dataA guarantee against a consumption higher than the model; the tablet's adaptive meter covers that
30 min lineFastest walk home in 30 min of emergency oxygenAn energy limit: time and energy are separate checks
Sand, bouldersWhat the orbital photo shows at 25 cmEvery patch: the sand map misses most sand, boulders under 1 m are invisible
RadioStraight line of sight on the 1 m terrain modelA link budget; real radios get some signal past a crest
Station valuesAn expert ranking from the literatureMeasurements of scientific return

The walk is simulated: there is no GPS on Mars. A real suit would locate itself with habitat and rover beacons and motion sensors.
