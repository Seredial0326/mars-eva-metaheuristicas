# Astrobiology and Mars-science priority frameworks for rock/mineral targets (clays vs carbonates vs sulfates, contacts, rover target prioritisation)

Purpose: judge whether the station ranking "clay-bearing ridge > carbonate/sulfate hollow > unit contact = previously-measured rover site" is defensible.

Source-access caveat (read first): several primary documents (Mars 2020 SDT report PDF >10 MB, iMOST PDF, Hays et al. 2017 on PMC, Des Marais 2013 GSA handout, Gaines et al. 2016) could not be machine-read in this session (size limit, CAPTCHA, or compressed PDF). Claims marked **[not re-verified]** come from the researcher's prior knowledge of these well-known papers, with DOI links, and should be spot-checked before going into the paper. Everything else was confirmed from a fetched page or a search-result excerpt.

## Q1. What do MEPAG / Mars 2020 SDT / iMOST / biosignature reviews say about clays vs carbonates vs sulfates? Is there an explicit ranking?

### Takeaway
No major framework (MEPAG Goals, Mars 2020 SDT, iMOST, NASA 2026 MEP objectives) gives an explicit ranked order "clays > carbonates > sulfates". They group phyllosilicates, carbonates, sulfates (plus silica and phosphates) together as aqueous-mineral targets with biosignature preservation potential. Where a preservation scale exists (Farmer and Des Marais lineage), silica and phosphates sit at the top, carbonates and fine-grained clay-rich mudstones in the middle-to-high range, and evaporitic sulfates/halides at the lower, less stable end. So "clay > carbonate/sulfate" has partial support. It is defensible for sulfates, but weak against carbonates, which are often ranked at least as high as clays.

### Cited Findings
**Frameworks (consensus documents)**
- MEPAG's Goals Document is organised in four tiers (goals, objectives, sub-objectives, investigations). The 2018 version includes a section on preserving biosignatures as evidence of past life. MEPAG updates it as discoveries warrant. — [MEPAG Goals Document 2018 (copy at NASA LaRC)](https://discovery.larc.nasa.gov/discovery/PDF_FILES/03a_MEPAG%20Goals_Document_2018.pdf)
- A newer MEPAG Goals Document (MEPAG, 2025) is cited as an input to NASA's 2026 Mars Exploration Program science objectives. That document's life/habitability theme aims to "determine the astrobiological potential of Mars and the processes that govern the preservation of organic and physical biosignatures". Its objectives cover extant life, modern habitability, ancient life, past habitability and organic chemistry. No mineral ranking appeared in the excerpt. — [NASA MEP Science Objectives 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/mep-science-objectives-2026b.pdf); [traceability table](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/mep-sciobj-table-tracing.pdf). Current MEPAG goals: https://www.lpi.usra.edu/mepag/goals (not fetched).
- The Mars 2020 SDT (Mustard et al. 2013) set mission objectives to "explore an astrobiologically relevant ancient environment on Mars", assess preservation potential at the site, look for potential biosignatures, and cache samples. — quoted in [NASA ECFRF report (Zaloumis)](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/psd/astrobiology/for-researchers/early-career-field-research-fund/report_lc_zaloumis_pr.pdf); SDT report itself: [Mars 2020 SDT Final Report](https://mepag.jpl.nasa.gov/reports/MEP/Mars_2020_SDT_Report_Final.pdf); [SDT Appendices](https://media2.spaceref.com/docs/2013/SDT_Appendices_Final_v6.pdf); [NASA Watch notice](https://nasawatch.com/exploration/mars-rover-2020-science-definition-team-report)
- A Des Marais and Mustard 2013 GSA presentation of the SDT framework uses a diagram of terrestrial biosignature preservation that puts "Halides, Sulfates" at the least-stable end and silica and phosphates at the most-stable end. It lists sedimentary carbonates among the candidate materials and "preservation enhanced in reducing conditions" as a pathway. — [Des Marais 2013 GSA handout](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2013AM/webprogram/Handout/Paper231327/DesMarais%20231327.pdf) (seen via search excerpt only; the PDF could not be parsed)
- A NASA (Ames) document says phosphates, carbonates, sulfates and phyllosilicates should all be sought for biosignature preservation. It treats them as a group, not ranked. — [NTRS 20180003291](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180003291/downloads/20180003291.pdf)
- iMOST (Beaty et al. 2019, 71 scientists) defined 7 objectives traceable to two decades of priorities. Objective 1 covers geologic processes and history with emphasis on water, and it maps specific sample types to objectives so the Mars 2020 team can choose. The search excerpts did not show the mineral-specific lists. — [iMOST full report (Caltech)](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/7rksv-ar292); [Glasgow eprint](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/182117/1/182117.pdf); [EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/556391)
- **[not re-verified]** iMOST Objective 2 (life) has sub-objectives for an ancient habitable environment with high biosignature preservation potential. Its sample types include lacustrine/fluvial fine-grained sediments, chemical sediments (carbonates, evaporites/sulfates, silica) and hydrothermal deposits, again without a strict mineral ranking. — [iMOST, MAPS 54, doi:10.1111/maps.13232](https://doi.org/10.1111/maps.13232)

**Review literature**
- Summons et al. 2011 (Mars Biosignature Working Group, chartered by the MSL Project Science Group) reviewed the formation and preservation of biosignatures on Mars. It judged biogenic organic molecules and biogenic gases to be the most definitive and detectable signatures, and stressed that the preservation processes were poorly understood. — [Summons et al. 2011, Astrobiology 11(2):157–181, doi:10.1089/ast.2010.0506](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/66519); [Carnegie page](https://carnegiescience.edu/preservation-martian-organic-and-environmental-records-final-report-mars-biosignature-working-0)
- **[not re-verified]** Summons et al. 2011 highlight fine-grained, clay-rich mudstones deposited rapidly under reducing conditions as favourable for organic preservation, because clays adsorb organics and lower permeability. They warn that oxidising, acidic or sulfate-rich brines and later diagenesis degrade organics. — same DOI
- Farmer and Des Marais 1999 set the "fossil record" exploration strategy. Their related work argues that the oldest body fossils on Earth are preserved in fine-grained siliceous sediments (cherts), and it names thermal-spring deposits as key targets. Farmer is quoted on microfossils in rapidly precipitated carbonate tufa. — [Farmer & Des Marais 1999, JGR 104(E11), doi:10.1029/1998JE000540](https://earthref.org/ERR/61222); [NTRS 1994 abstract](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19940030919); [SpaceDaily 1999](https://www.spacedaily.com/news/mars-life-99b.html)
- **[not re-verified]** In Farmer and Des Marais 1999, the preservation potential of mineral matrices depends on rapid entombment, fine grain size, low permeability and long crustal residence time (chemical stability). Silica, phosphate and carbonate rank as favourable, clay-rich shales also rank well, and evaporites (sulfates, halides) are more soluble and prone to recrystallisation. — same DOI
- Hays et al. 2017 (Astrobiology 17(4):363–400, from a 2016 workshop) assessed habitability and preservation potential for 5 analog settings: hydrothermal, subaqueous, subaerial, subsurface, iron-rich. It organises the problem by depositional environment, not by mineral class. — [UNLV record](https://digitalscholarship.unlv.edu/geo_fac_articles/252); [PMC5478115](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5478115/)

**Sulfates: mixed evidence**
- A SETI Institute study of sulfate evaporites found that capture and preservation of organic matter (as kerogen) is higher in matrix-dominated, clay-rich subfacies and lower where sulfate crystals grew early within the sediment. This is direct support for clay > sulfate within evaporitic settings. — [NAI 2015 Annual Report (SETI)](https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/nai/annual-reports/2015/seti/biosignature-capture-and-preservation-in-sulfate-evaporite-deposits/index.html)
- Ca-sulfates from Tírez Lake (Spain) are an effective host for organic preservation, but they mask organic functional groups in spectroscopic analysis. — search excerpt in [Summons-related search results; see PMC6987737](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6987737)
- Benison et al. (JGR Planets, 2024, doi:10.1029/2023JE008155): the Hogwallow Flats and Yori Pass sulfate-rich fine sandstones in the Jezero fan front contain phyllosilicates, hematite, Ca-, Fe-Mg- and ferric sulfates and possibly chlorides. The sulfates are both detrital and diagenetic. Fluid and solid inclusions could preserve biosignatures and environmental records, so the cores Hazeltop, Bearwallow and Kukaklek are flagged for analysis on Earth. — [summary page](https://cosmochemistry-papers.com/2024/02/21/depositional-and-diagenetic-sulfates-of-hogwallow-flats-and-yori-pass-jezero-crater-evaluating-preservation-potential-of-environmental-indicators-and-possible-biosignatures-from-past-martian-surface/)
- A companion Hogwallow Flats diagenesis paper finds that phyllosilicates, sulfate grains and cements, and mottling may all be sites of enhanced organic preservation. — [astrobiology.com summary](https://astrobiology.com/2024/12/10/diagenetic-history-and-biosignature-preservation-potential-of-fine-grained-rocks-at-hogwallow-flats-jezero-crater-mars/)

**Clays: not uncritically "best"**
- A 2019 ExoMars landing-site abstract notes that the organic preservation potential of Noachian clay sediments and paleosols is "less constrained". — [EPSC-DPS2019-445](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EPSC-DPS2019/EPSC-DPS2019-445-1.pdf)
- **[not re-verified]** Curiosity found the first in situ evidence of preserved organic molecules (thiophenes, aromatics) in the ~3.5 Ga clay-bearing Murray mudstones in Gale. — [Eigenbrode et al. 2018, Science 360:1096, doi:10.1126/science.aas9185](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aas9185)

**Carbonates**
- Popular coverage of Jezero site selection says hydrated silica, like carbonates, can preserve fossils. This is a secondary source. — [Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/mars-2020-rover-is-going-to-a-place-on-mars-thats-perfect-for-preserving-fossils)

### Inferences
- The consensus documents treat clays, carbonates and sulfates as one tier of "aqueous-mineral, potentially habitable, preservation-capable" targets. Differences come from the depositional setting (fine-grained, rapidly buried, reducing, little diagenesis), not from the mineral label alone.
- Ranking clay above sulfate is defensible: sulfates are lower on the Farmer/Des Marais stability scale, they record evaporative and often acidic/saline water (lower water activity), and the SETI data show lower organic capture where sulfate grew early.
- Ranking clay above carbonate is debatable. Carbonates are usually ranked as high as or higher than clays for preservation (rapid entombment of microbes, isotopic records), and they were a key driver of Jezero site selection. A defensible formulation is "clay ≈ carbonate > sulfate", or a ranking justified by context (fine-grained lacustrine clay versus a wind-deflated hollow of uncertain origin).
- For a boxwork terrain specifically, ridges are usually interpreted as fracture-filling cemented material. Their "clay-bearing" label should be backed by orbital spectra (CRISM). Otherwise the ridge's mineralogy, which could be sulfate or other cement, is itself uncertain.

### Gaps
- Exact text of the Mars 2020 SDT report and of iMOST Objective 2's sample-type tables could not be read (PDF size or parsing limits).
- The latest MEPAG Goals Document (2025) was not fetched, so it is unconfirmed whether it contains any mineral-specific ranking. Probably none, but unconfirmed.
- No quantitative cross-mineral comparison of organic preservation on Mars was found.

## Q2. Do frameworks value geologic contacts / stratigraphic transitions (e.g., the Gale clay–sulfate transition) as high-priority targets?

### Takeaway
Yes, and strongly. Stratigraphic transitions and mineral diversity were an explicit reason for choosing Gale (clay-to-sulfate sequence in Mount Sharp) and Jezero (delta, carbonate margin, crater floor, rim). Perseverance samples are explicitly chosen to "represent the stratigraphy" of each campaign. Contacts record environmental change (e.g., wetter to drier), which a single unit cannot. Ranking a contact at the bottom therefore conflicts with how MSL and Mars 2020 actually reason, unless the contact is poorly exposed or not inside a mineralogically distinct sequence.

### Cited Findings
- Curiosity went to Gale because orbital data showed layered rocks with different minerals in Mount Sharp. The goal is to trace how habitability changed as the environment dried, going from clay-bearing up into sulfate-bearing rock. Clays form where water is present and sulfates where water evaporates, and the transition is read as a major environmental change. — [Smithsonian NASM, "10 years of Curiosity"](https://www.airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/10-years-curiosity-mars)
- "Higher on the mountain, clay minerals give way to sulfate-bearing sandstones … Such a change reflects the gradual drying of the planet as its atmosphere thinned and surface water became scarce." — [Caltech Magazine](https://magazine.caltech.edu/post/habitability-on-mars-rovers)
- Mount Sharp stratigraphy records alternating wet and dry depositional environments. — [Rapin et al. 2021 (Caltech record)](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20210412-083509911); [HAL](https://hal-insu.archives-ouvertes.fr/insu-03198811)
- Later diagenetic fluids partly reset the clay record in the transition. Altered zones remain relevant for habitability (saline diagenetic fluids). — [Seeger 2025 Caltech thesis ch. 4](https://thesis.caltech.edu/17382/05/Seeger_Christina_2025_Thesis_Ch4.pdf)
- CheMin found crystalline Mg-sulfate absent from drill samples in the transition. Explanations: sulfate concentrated in concretions the drill missed, or X-ray-amorphous sulfate. So orbital "sulfate" labels do not always match the drilled rock. — [Goldschmidt 2022 abstract](https://goldschmidtabstracts.info/2022/13213.pdf)
- **[not re-verified]** The clay-to-sulfate stratigraphy of Mount Sharp, seen from orbit, was proposed as a global record of Mars' environmental transition and was central to the selection of Gale. — [Milliken, Grotzinger & Thomson 2010, GRL 37, doi:10.1029/2009GL041870](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009GL041870); [Grotzinger et al. 2012, Space Sci. Rev. 170, doi:10.1007/s11214-012-9892-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-012-9892-2)
- Perseverance "collected samples representing the stratigraphy of each of four campaigns". The crater-floor samples form a suite of igneous rocks with variable aqueous alteration, the fan-front samples capture fluvial to deltaic sediments, and the overall goal is "a suite of samples preserving a diversity of formation environments and ages". — [Herd et al. 2025, PNAS (Caltech PDF)](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/jq5a7-x1736/preview/herd-et-al-2025-sampling-mars-geologic-context-and-preliminary-characterization-of-samples-collected-by-the-nasa-mars.pdf)
- The notional Jezero sample collection includes crater-floor units, fine- and coarse-grained delta facies, chemical sediments that could preserve biosignatures, rim bedrock, and regolith. A 2024 study prioritises sites outside Jezero (Monument Valley, NW rim) for their exposed layered basement. — [Scheller et al. 2024 LPSC (NTRS)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240000395/downloads/Scheller_etal_Jan2024_LPSC_OutsideJezero.pdf)
- "Cheyava Falls"/"Sapphire Canyon", the sample with potential biosignatures (Nature, Sept 2025), came from outcrops on the edge of Neretva Vallis, an inflow channel. — [NASA Mars 2020 mission page](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/)

### Inferences
- Contacts and transitions are valued because they record changes in water chemistry and climate, and they constrain relative timing. That is iMOST Objective 1 (geologic history with emphasis on water) and a core MEPAG Goal III-type question.
- A contact between a clay unit and a sulfate unit is arguably *more* valuable than either unit alone, because it brackets the habitability change. Giving "unit contact" the lowest rank is the weakest part of the proposed ordering.
- A defensible alternative: rank by "number of independent questions answered". A clay–sulfate contact can then rank at or near the top, a pure clay ridge and a carbonate/sulfate hollow next, and a previously measured site lowest for new science but useful for calibration.

### Gaps
- No framework gives a numerical weight to contacts versus single units. The evidence is qualitative (site-selection rationales, sampling logic).
- Whether the specific boxwork-station "contact" sits between mineralogically distinct units is a local question, not covered by the literature.

## Q3. How do MSL / Mars 2020 teams prioritise targets day to day, and how do they value revisiting / ground-truthing known sites?

### Takeaway
Both rovers use a two-level process. Long-term (strategic) planners set campaign goals and the route. The daily tactical team, organised by science theme groups, picks contact and remote-sensing targets within resource constraints, reaching consensus in a Science Operations Working Group. Perseverance adds formal sample selection: each core is documented with a standard set of observations and, in hindsight, scored against MSR objectives in a Sample Science Traceability Matrix that rewards both individual value and complementarity across the suite. Revisiting known sites is mainly valued for context, calibration and comparison (e.g., the standardised "abrasion patch" proximity science before coring). It is not treated as a primary discovery target. Ranking it at the bottom for *new* science is consistent with practice.

### Cited Findings
- MSL daily planning starts "with strategic guidance from our long term planners". The geology and environmental theme leads then guide the science team in building the plan. "Identifying and vetting contact science targets for APXS and MAHLI is typically the first order of business". Rover planners check arm reachability, and science–RP mediators iterate when a target is too hard. On drive days, surface-property scientists help choose safe, efficient routes. — [NASA Curiosity blog, sols 4001–4003](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/mars-science-laboratory/sols-4001-4003-the-ones-who-make-curiosity-go/)
- Archived MSL documentation: Science Theme Group leads present their plans at a Science Operations Working Group (SOWG). The chair builds a consensus plan that meets resource constraints and the Long Term Planners' strategic direction. — [MSL Science Corner, science operations (archived 2013)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130214172824/http://msl-scicorner.jpl.nasa.gov/scienceoperations); [science planning (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130215012759/http://msl-scicorner.jpl.nasa.gov/scienceplanning)
- MSL moved to campaign-based operations (planning across multi-sol campaigns instead of single sols). — [Gaines et al. 2016 ICAPS, co-author Vasavada](https://www-robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/media/documents/gaines-icaps2016-rover.pdf); [Gaines et al. 2017 IWPSS](https://ai.jpl.nasa.gov/public/documents/papers/gaines-iwpss2017-expressing.pdf)
- **[not re-verified]** MSL Science Theme Groups have included Geology, Mineralogy & Geochemistry, Environment, and Engineering/Surface properties. — [Grotzinger et al. 2012, doi:10.1007/s11214-012-9892-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-012-9892-2)
- Perseverance: "Once a sampling target has been identified, each sample is documented by a standardized set of required activities and observations". The documentation goes to the PDS. A Sample Science Traceability Matrix (Herd et al., Astrobiology, Oct 2025) offers "a structured, quantitative method to assess each sample's capacity to address key scientific questions, while highlighting synergies across the sample suite". — [NTRS 20250009654](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250009654/downloads/20250009654.pdf); [Herd et al. 2025 PNAS](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/jq5a7-x1736/preview/herd-et-al-2025-sampling-mars-geologic-context-and-preliminary-characterization-of-samples-collected-by-the-nasa-mars.pdf)
- Return-sample objectives for this matrix are grouped under four primary objectives, beginning with "Geologic History – Reconstruct the formation and alteration history" of the samples. — [NTRS 20250009654](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250009654/downloads/20250009654.pdf)
- Between sols 109 and 1,088, 27 tubes were sealed (including witness tubes). As of April 2025, 8 sample tubes and 2 witness tubes remained. Tube scarcity forces strict prioritisation. — [Herd et al. 2025](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/jq5a7-x1736/preview/herd-et-al-2025-sampling-mars-geologic-context-and-preliminary-characterization-of-samples-collected-by-the-nasa-mars.pdf); [National Geographic 2025](https://nationalgeographic.com/science/article/mars-rock-sample-return-life-mystery)
- **[not re-verified]** Mars 2020 mission-level sampling goals: a scientifically selected, well-documented, returnable cache that includes samples with the highest biosignature preservation potential and samples representing the geologic diversity of the site. — [Farley et al. 2020, Space Sci. Rev. 216:142, doi:10.1007/s11214-020-00762-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-020-00762-y)

### Inferences
- Real rover prioritisation combines (i) the strategic campaign goal, (ii) scientific value, (iii) diversity and complementarity relative to what is already measured, and (iv) engineering cost (reachability, drive, time, data volume). This maps well onto a value-weighted orienteering formulation (E5): station value set by strategy and science, edge cost by traverse.
- The complementarity logic in the traceability matrix supports *discounting* a previously measured site. Its marginal information gain is lower. It is still non-zero, since a known site serves as ground truth and calibration for orbital classification (e.g., boxwork mapping from HiRISE/CRISM).
- Ranking "unit contact" equal to "previously measured site" contradicts practice. Contacts are high-information targets in strategic planning; known sites are calibration targets.

### Gaps
- No public, explicit numerical scoring rule used tactically by MSL/Mars 2020 for individual targets was found. The traceability matrix is the closest, and its scoring details were not read.
- How the team weights "revisit for ground truth" against "new target" is not documented in the sources found. The evidence is indirect (complementarity, standardised proximity science).
- The current (2024–2026) MSL theme-group names and structure may differ from the archived 2013 page.
